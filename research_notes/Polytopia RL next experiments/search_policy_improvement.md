# Turning test-time search gains into a permanently stronger policy (search-based policy improvement, expert iteration, AlphaZero-style distillation)

Scope: methods and evidence on distilling search into a policy/value network, why naive imitation of search-chosen actions fails, and what to do instead — with attention to few-simulation regimes, sampled (whole-turn) candidate sets, imperfect information, and the specific Polytopia failure mode (search overrides the policy 87% of the time by margins below its own score noise; imitation gave +0 Elo; value-head refit lost most of the search gain).

Sources are primary papers where possible; the Gumbel results are quoted from Danihelka's 2023 UCL thesis (which reproduces the ICLR 2022 paper chapter verbatim, with figure numbering 5.x / C.x) because OpenReview blocked automated fetching.

---

## Q1. AlphaZero / MuZero policy targets: why full visit-count distributions (soft targets) rather than argmax actions, and target quality vs number of simulations

### Takeaway
Every lineage that measured it found that a soft target over all searched actions beats a hard "search-chosen action" target (ExIt: +50 +-13 Elo for identical top-1 accuracy; Gumbel: the "simple" argmax loss is strictly worse than the completed-Q loss at every simulation budget), and that visit-count targets degrade into noise when simulations are few: with n <= 16 simulations plain MuZero fails to learn 9x9 Go at all, and with n <= 4 it fails on Ms Pacman.

### Cited Findings
- AlphaGo-Zero/AlphaZero/MuZero train the policy toward the root visit-count distribution; MuZero samples actions "proportional to the visit count for each action from the root node", uses 800 simulations per search in board games and 50 in Atari, and value targets are the game outcome (board games) or n=10-step bootstrapped returns (Atari) — [MuZero, Schrittwieser et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265)
- ExIt (Anthony, Tian, Barber 2017) compared Chosen-Action Targets (CAT: loss = -log pi(a*|s), a* = argmax visit count) against Tree-Policy Targets (TPT: match n(s,a)/n(s), with n(s)=10,000). Top-1 prediction error was essentially identical (47.0% CAT vs 47.7% TPT; top-3 65.4% vs 65.7%) yet the TPT network was "50 +-13 Elo stronger". Their explanation: TPT is cost-sensitive — when MCTS is unsure between two moves it "penalises misclassifications less severely", and future experts use evaluations of all moves, not just the top one — [ExIt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439)
- Grill et al. 2020 give three reasons visit counts are poor targets at low budgets: a newly found high-value leaf needs many simulations before it shows up in visit counts; with few simulations the visit distribution is a ratio of small integers and cannot express fine-grained policies; actions needing a large budget to be sampled even once may never be explored since selection is deterministic and the prior is trained only on visited actions. They write that "the discretization noise in pi_hat makes it a worse training target than pi_bar" at low budgets — [Grill et al. 2020, MCTS as regularized policy optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12509)
- Danihelka et al.: "In the 9x9 Go results, MuZero fails to learn from 16 or fewer simulations. Strikingly, Gumbel MuZero learns reliably even with 2 simulations" (training with n in {2,4,16,32,200}, evaluated at 800 simulations, 2 seeds) — [Danihelka thesis ch. 5 = ICLR 2022 paper](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf); paper page: [OpenReview bERaNdoegnO](https://openreview.net/forum?id=bERaNdoegnO)
- Atari (Ms Pacman, n in {2,4,16,18,50}): "MuZero fails to learn from 4 or fewer simulations"; Gumbel MuZero "again learns reliably even with n = 2 simulations" — [Danihelka thesis Fig. 5.5](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- The "simple policy loss" L_simple = -log pi(A_{n+1}) (imitate the search-chosen action) is ablated against the completed-Q loss in Fig. 5.3a / Fig. C.1 at n = 2, 4, 16, 200; the completed-Q loss (which "trains all actions, not only the action A_{n+1}") is the one used in all main results, and "Without the completed Q-values, TRPO MuZero and MPO MuZero would fail to produce a policy improvement" — [Danihelka thesis](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- MuZero (2020) showed that with n=6 simulations per move, "fewer than the number of actions", MuZero still learned Ms Pacman (8 actions) effectively when evaluated at 50 sims — [MuZero Fig. 3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265). (Note the conflict with the 2022 Gumbel result that MuZero fails at n<=4 on the same game; the 2020 runs used n>=6 so the two are not strictly inconsistent.)
- KataGo: "the search usually does not deviate much from the policy prior, so the policy does not readily improve" at low playout counts; informal research (Forsten 2019) suggests the ideal playout count for policy learning is much larger than for value learning, "not far from AlphaZero's choice of 800 playouts per move" — [KataGo, Wu 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10565)
- Soemers et al. 2019 argue the cross-entropy-to-visit-counts target transfers MCTS's exploratory behaviour into the policy (undesirable), and instead use a tree-search policy gradient that sums over all actions: g = (1/|B|) sum_s sum_a grad pi(s,a) Q^M(s,a) with Q^M from the search's successor values; this pi_tspg "consistently outperforms" the cross-entropy policy in play strength — [Soemers et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05809)

### Inferences
- The Polytopia +0 Elo result is the ExIt-CAT / Gumbel-"simple loss" failure mode in its extreme form: when the search's chosen turn is decided by margins below the critic's noise, the hard label is approximately a sample from the policy itself (its top-1 "accuracy" is already ~random), so cross-entropy on it reproduces pi, and the KL anchor to the parent further pins it there. A soft target that carries the relative scores of all 16 candidates is the minimum change every reference lineage made.
- "Soft" alone is not sufficient: the visit-count soft target at 16 sims is itself discretization noise (Grill). What worked at n in {2..16} is a target built from Q-values (completed Q), not from counts.

### Gaps
- No paper reports Elo numbers in a table for Gumbel vs MuZero at each simulation count; results are figures (9x9 Go Elo curves, 2 seeds). The thesis itself says "We make no claims about confidence intervals."

## Q2. Gumbel MuZero (Danihelka et al. ICLR 2022): the guaranteed-improvement target with few simulations — exact formulas, how many simulations suffice, applicability to sampled whole-turn candidates

### Takeaway
Gumbel MuZero replaces visit counts with an explicit improved policy pi' = softmax(logits + sigma(completedQ)), where unvisited actions get the value v_pi (zero advantage) and sigma(q) = (c_visit + max_b N(b)) * c_scale * q; the root picks the action maximizing g(a) + logits(a) + sigma(q_hat(a)) among m = min(n,16) Gumbel-top-k sampled candidates refined by Sequential Halving. This is provably a policy improvement "if the visited actions are correctly evaluated", learns 9x9 Go with n = 2 simulations, and the m-sampled-candidates structure maps directly onto "16 sampled whole turns" — the candidate set IS the Gumbel-top-k sample.

### Cited Findings
- Problem statement: "The problem becomes interesting when the number of possible actions is larger than the number of simulations, i.e., when k > n. For example, 19x19 Go has 362 possible actions, and we will do experiments with as few as n = 2 simulations." Policy improvement is defined as E[q(A_{n+1})] >= sum_a pi(a) q(a) — [thesis sec. 5.3](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Counterexample showing that "acting with the best action from the top-n most-probable actions fails to produce a policy improvement": q=(0,0,1), pi=(0.5,0.3,0.2), n=2 gives E[q(A_{n+1})]=0 < 0.2. "If the number of simulations is smaller than the number of actions, then UCT and AlphaZero do not ensure a policy improvement." — [thesis sec. 5.3.2](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Root algorithm (Algorithm 5, "Sequential Halving with Gumbel"): sample k Gumbel variables g ~ Gumbel(0); A_topm = argtop(g + logits, m) (m actions sampled without replacement via the Gumbel-top-k trick); run Sequential Halving with n simulations over A_topm, comparing g(a) + logits(a) + sigma(q_hat(a)); return A_{n+1} = argmax_{a in Remaining}(g(a) + logits(a) + sigma(q_hat(a))). Sequential Halving splits the n simulations equally over log2(m) phases, visiting all remaining actions equally in each phase and rejecting half after each phase. Illustrated with n = 200, m = 16 — [thesis sec. 5.3.3-5.3.4](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Proof sketch (Appendix C.1): by the Gumbel-Max trick, E_{A~pi}[q(A)] = E_g[q(argmax_a (g(a)+logits(a)))]; since A_topn contains the argmax of g+logits, restricting to A_topn loses nothing; then selecting argmax over A_topn of g + logits + sigma(q) with a monotone sigma can only raise expected q. Hence E[q(A_{n+1})] >= E_{A~pi}[q(A)] — [thesis App. C.1](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- sigma: "sigma(q_hat(a)) = (c_visit + max_b N(b)) c_scale q_hat(a), (5.8) where max_b N(b) is the visit count of the most-visited action. The transformation progressively increases the scale for q_hat(a) and reduces the effect of the prior policy. This scale is inspired by the policy updates in MPO ... Experimentally, c_visit = 50, c_scale = 1.0 allowed us to use the same hyperparameters even if changing the number of simulations." — [thesis eq. 5.8](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Policy target (eqs. 5.10-5.12): completedQ(a) = q(a) if N(a) > 0, else v_pi, with v_pi = sum_a pi(a) q(a) (approximated by the value net / mixed estimator); pi' = softmax(logits + sigma(completedQ)); L_completed(pi) = KL(pi', pi). "Intuitively, the completed Q-values give zero advantage to the unvisited actions." The alternative "simple policy loss" L_simple = -log pi(A_{n+1}) is strictly the hard-label baseline — [thesis sec. 5.4](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Mixed-value estimator for unvisited actions (mctx `qtransform_completed_by_mix_value`): v_mix = (raw_value + N * weighted_q) / (N + 1) with N = sum of visit counts and weighted_q = sum_{visited} pi(a) q(a) / sum_{visited} pi(a); completed_q = where(N(a) > 0, q(a), v_mix); then min-max rescale to [0,1] and multiply by (maxvisit_init + max_a N(a)) * value_scale, defaults value_scale = 0.1, maxvisit_init = 50, rescale_values = True, use_mixed_value = True. Fig. C.1: completing with v_mix "works better than Q-values completed by the raw value network" — [mctx qtransforms.py](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mctx/blob/main/mctx/_src/qtransforms.py); [thesis Fig. C.1](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- mctx `gumbel_muzero_policy` defaults: max_num_considered_actions = 16, gumbel_scale = 1.0 (docstring: "Evaluation on perfect-information games can use gumbel_scale=0.0"), and the returned training target is `action_weights = softmax(prior_logits + completed_qvalues)` with invalid actions masked — [mctx policies.py](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mctx/blob/main/mctx/_src/policies.py)
- Non-root (interior) deterministic action selection: argmax_a [pi'(a) - N(a) / (1 + sum_b N(b))] (eq. 5.14), derived as the action minimising squared error between pi' and the resulting normalized visit counts; "We recommend a deterministic action selection only for non-root nodes. At the root node, the Gumbel noise is helpful for trying different actions in different episodes, while ensuring an improved expected value." Full Gumbel MuZero (this rule at non-root) is shown only when significantly different from Gumbel MuZero (i.e., it rarely matters) — [thesis sec. 5.5, Fig. C.2](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Number of sampled candidates: "When sampling m = 4 actions without replacement, the simulation budget is spent on the small number of actions. The learning was then slower. In all other Go experiments, we sample m = min(n,16) actions without replacement" (ablation m in {4,8,16,32} at n=200). In Atari "we sample m = min(n,18) actions ... In the experiments with n <= 18, Gumbel MuZero selects A_{n+1} from the n visited actions, without any Sequential Halving. This confirms that planning with Gumbel is the key ingredient responsible for the policy improvement from a small number of simulations." — [thesis sec. 5.7](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Sampling with vs without replacement: an ablation "Gumbel MuZero sampled with replacement (Replacement)" samples the m actions with replacement "as in Sampled MuZero (Hubert et al., 2021)" (Fig. 5.2) — [thesis sec. 5.7](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Scale sensitivity: on Go "Any c_visit >= 50 produced similar results"; on Atari Q-values are normalized by max(v_hat, max_a q_hat) - min(v_hat, min_a q_hat) to [-1,1] and then scaled with c_visit = 50, c_scale = 0.1 so that a scaled advantage is "approximately in [-5,5]"; on beam rider (partially observable) "performance is worse ... if using large c_scale. This indicates that g(a) + logits(a) are helpful in the argmax" (c_scale in {0.01,0.1,1,10,100}). The bounded advantage gives a bounded total-variation distance between pi and pi' (Muesli Theorem 4.4.1: max TV = tanh(c/2) for clipping threshold c) — [thesis sec. 5.7.3, App. C.5](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Large scale: with n = 400 simulations Gumbel MuZero "reaches or exceeds" MuZero on 19x19 Go and Gumbel AlphaZero matches AlphaZero on chess (Fig. 5.4) — [thesis sec. 5.7.2](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Compute: Table C.1 training-step speedups on 9x9 Go from fewer simulations when not learner-bound: n=32: 5.9x, n=16: 11.3x, n=8: 16.2x, n=4: 24.3x (vs n=200) — [thesis Table C.1](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Evaluation sims matter separately from training sims: Fig. C.4 shows a net trained with n=2 and evaluated with 800/200/32/16/2/1 simulations ("n = 1 simulation acts with the most-probable action from the policy network") — [thesis Fig. C.4](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Follow-ups confirm the structure generalises to combinatorial/joint actions ("Multiagent Gumbel MuZero", AAAI 2024) and to exploration variants ("Agents Explore the Environment Beyond Good Actions", 2023, which names the "Full Gumbel" deterministic non-root variant) — [Multiagent Gumbel MuZero](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1609/aaai.v38i11.29121); [arXiv 2306.03408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03408)

### Inferences
- Mapping to Polytopia's turn search: 16 sampled candidate turns drawn from the policy are, up to the with/without-replacement detail, exactly the Gumbel-top-k candidate set A_topm with m = 16. Each candidate gets one "visit" (one opponent-reply rollout + critic score), so max_b N(b) = 1 and sigma(q) = (50 + 1) * c_scale * q_normalized. The correct target is pi'(turn_i) proportional to exp(logits_i + sigma(q_i)) over the 16 candidates (plus the implicit "all other turns" mass at zero advantage), where logits_i is the sequence log-probability of the whole turn under the current policy, and the distillation loss is a sequence-level KL: sum_i pi'(turn_i) * (-log pi_theta(turn_i)), i.e., advantage-weighted sequence imitation with weights bounded by the scale.
- Because the guarantee holds only "if the visited actions are correctly evaluated", critic noise is the direct threat: the improvement is roughly E over candidates of pi'-weighted advantage, and with 16 one-shot evaluations the realized advantage is partly noise. The remedy inside the Gumbel design is the scale: c_scale should be set so that sigma(q) spreads logits by roughly the critic's true signal, not more (the beam-rider ablation shows over-trusting q hurts in a partially observable game). A second remedy is to spend simulations to raise max N (Sequential Halving over the 16 candidates: e.g., 16 -> 8 -> 4 -> 2 with repeated determinizations/rollouts per survivor), which both reduces noise on the survivors and legitimately increases the sigma scale.
- The hard-label (+0 Elo) result is predicted by the paper's own ablation ordering: L_simple < L_completed at every n, and most dramatically at small n.

### Gaps
- The paper does not run experiments on sampled whole-sequence candidates (macro-actions); the whole-turn mapping above is an inference, though Sampled MuZero (Q3) supplies the formal justification for sample-based targets.
- No published study quantifies how much critic noise (relative to true advantage spread) the completed-Q target tolerates before improvement vanishes.

## Q3. Expert Iteration (Anthony et al. 2017) and Sampled MuZero (Hubert et al. 2021): forming targets from a sampled subset of actions

### Takeaway
Sampled MuZero gives the formal recipe for a policy-improvement target over K sampled actions: Î_beta pi(a) = (beta_hat/beta)(a) * f(s,a,Z) with beta_hat the empirical sample distribution; for the MPO-style instance with beta = pi this reduces to Î pi(a) = beta_hat(a) exp(Q(s,a)/tau) / Z — i.e., softmax over the sampled candidates of Q/tau (sequence log-prob cancels when the proposal is the policy). K = 3-5 already suffices in low-dimensional cases; K = 50 of 362 approaches full-action-space performance in Go; K = 2 was insufficient on Atari. ExIt's loop (sample states with the apprentice, label with the expert, aggregate DAgger-style, rebuild the expert) plus its TPT-over-CAT result is the historical template.

### Cited Findings
- Sampled MuZero: sample K actions from a proposal beta (sec. 5.4 "sample K<<N actions from a distribution beta"); empirical distribution beta_hat(a|s) = (1/K) sum_i delta_{a,a_i}; sample-based improvement operator Î_beta pi(a|s) = (beta_hat/beta)(a|s) * f(s,a,Z_beta(s)); for MPO with beta = pi: Î_beta pi(a|s) = beta_hat(a|s) exp(Q(s,a)/tau) / Z_beta(s). In the MCTS instance the target is the root visit distribution over the sampled actions, N(s,a)/sum_b N(s,b), and search uses the modified prior pi_hat_beta proportional to (beta_hat/beta) * pi inside PUCT; projection minimises KL(Î pi_beta || pi_theta) (a cross-entropy variant CE = E_{a~Î pi}[-log pi_theta(a)] is also given). beta is set to pi "potentially modulated by a temperature parameter", with Dirichlet noise added to beta and pi at the root — [Sampled MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06303)
- Sample counts: Go sweep K in {15,25,50,100} of 362 actions, "K=50 samples already closely approaching the performance of the baseline" (baseline anchored at 2000 Elo; curves only, no table); Atari (18 actions) "Searching with K=2 samples is not sufficient", K=3 approaches baseline; humanoid.run: K=3 "already sufficient", K=5 works; K=20 used for DM Control / RWRL. Appendix A.5: using pi_hat_beta instead of pi matters most when K is small — [Sampled MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06303)
- Sampled MuZero does not discuss sampling with/without replacement; Danihelka et al. ablate it (Gumbel-top-k is without replacement; "Replacement" variant mimics Sampled MuZero) — [Sampled MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06303); [thesis Fig. 5.2](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- ExIt loop (Alg. 1): sample self-play states with the current apprentice; label each with the expert's imitation target; train a new apprentice; rebuild the expert from the new apprentice. Online ExIt aggregates all past datasets "similar to the DAgger algorithm"; removing the expert-improvement step "reduces it to DAgger". Expert = 10,000-simulation N-MCTS with value-net weight w_v = 0.75 mixed with rollouts. Value target is binary cross-entropy against the sampled game result z (the true expert value V^{pi*} was too expensive, so V^{pi_hat} is used). Results: 75.3% vs MoHex (10k iterations), 59.3% vs MoHex at 100k, 55.6% vs MoHex at 4 s/move — [ExIt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439)
- ExIt policy target comparison (CAT vs TPT): identical top-1 error, +50 +-13 Elo for TPT (see Q1) — [ExIt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439)
- KataGo "forced playouts and policy target pruning": each root child with any playouts gets at least n_forced(c) = sqrt(k * P(c) * sum N) forced playouts (k = 2), then those extra playouts are subtracted from the policy target unless PUCT would have chosen the move anyway, "decoupling the policy target in AlphaZero from the dynamics of MCTS or the use of explorative noise"; ablation speedup 1.25x — [KataGo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10565)

### Inferences
- With candidates sampled from the policy itself (beta = pi), Sampled MuZero's operator says the target over the 16 turns is softmax_i(Q_i / tau) — the sequence log-probs cancel through beta_hat/beta — while the Gumbel operator keeps the logits inside the softmax (softmax(logits + sigma(Q))). Both are legitimate; the Gumbel form is the more conservative (prior-regularized) one and is the one that was shown to work at n = 2-16. Either way, the target is a *weighting of sampled turns by exponentiated critic score*, not a one-hot.
- Importance correction matters: if candidates are drawn from a tempered or noised proposal (as in Polytopia's sampled turns at temperature), the beta_hat/beta factor must be applied or the target will be biased toward the proposal's exploration.
- ExIt's TPT-vs-CAT Elo gap at identical top-1 accuracy is direct evidence that "the search mostly agrees with the policy so there is nothing to learn" is wrong: the information is in the ranking/margins of the non-chosen actions.

### Gaps
- No source evaluates K = 16 sampled macro-actions (multi-step turns) specifically; the closest are Go with K = 15-25 of 362 and "Multiagent Gumbel MuZero" for factorized joint actions.

## Q4. MuZero Reanalyse / value targets from search; "search as teacher for the value function" vs "for the policy"; what breaks when the value head is refit

### Takeaway
Reanalyse re-runs search on stored states to regenerate the *policy* target (fresh visit distribution, 80% of updates in MuZero), while *value* targets remain n-step bootstrapped returns (n reduced from 10 to 5, value loss weight reduced to 0.25); regressing the value head directly to the search's root value was measured to be worse (0-step search-value target: median 115.3% vs 126.6% for TD-5 at 20M frames, 99.5% reanalyse). KataGo keeps game outcome as the main value target with short-term MCTS-value heads only as auxiliaries, and Willemsen et al. show the MCTS root value ("soft-Z") is permanently biased by exploration.

### Cited Findings
- MuZero Appendix H (Reanalyze): re-running search with the latest parameters "produces a fresh policy target, which is used for 80% of updates"; a target network provides the n-step bootstrapped value target; samples per state rose from 0.1 to 2.0; the value target loss weight dropped to 0.25 (vs 1.0 for policy and reward); n-step return reduced from n=10 to n=5 — [MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265)
- MuZero Unplugged (Schrittwieser et al. 2021): Reanalyse policy target = MCTS visit distribution from a fresh search; value target in Atari = 5-step TD toward a target network (DM Control: direct regression to the MCTS value); reward = observed. Table 8 (Atari, 20M frames, 99.5% reanalyse): 5-step TD median 126.6% / mean 450.6% vs 0-step (search value) median 115.3% / mean 385.8%. Authors chose TD-5 because "the gain from bootstrapping" with observed rewards outweighed off-policy harm. Reanalyse fractions: 50% @ 2000M frames median 1331.7%; 95% @ 200M median 1006.4%; 99.5% @ 20M median 126.6%. Offline Atari: MuZero Unplugged 265.3% median vs CRR 155.6% vs BC 53.3% — [MuZero Unplugged](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294)
- MuZero Unplugged Table 4 (how to act after offline training, median Atari): policy-head acting with Reanalyse loss 203.2; value-head acting 239.9; MCTS acting 265.3 — i.e., even after distillation, acting with search retains a margin over the policy head — [MuZero Unplugged](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294)
- KataGo main value head predicts the final game result; auxiliary "short-term value" heads target (1-lambda) sum_{t'>=t} MCTS_value(t') lambda^{t'-t} with horizons of ~6, 16, 50 turns on 19x19; nets "seem to train slightly faster and achieve slightly better value and score loss on the main head" when the main loss weight is reduced and these auxiliaries added; a further "uncertainty" head predicts the squared error of the short-term value prediction — [KataGo methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)
- KataGo playout-cap rationale: the game outcome is "one noisy result per game", so value training benefits from many cheap games, whereas policy training benefits from larger searches — [KataGo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10565)
- Willemsen, Baier, Kaisers 2021: define a 3-D family of AlphaZero value targets including the original outcome z, soft-Z (y_softZ(s) = V_MCTS(s,s), the search root value), A0C, and their A0GB (greedy backup); soft-Z "is also biased by exploratory moves during the MCTS" and the bias "is permanent and associated with the difference between the actual and ideal target policy"; in a tabular deep-exploration gridworld (100 MCTS sims/move) A0GB finds the optimal policy where the original target does not; on Connect-Four and 6x6 Breakthrough soft-Z, A0C and A0GB "achieve better performance and faster training than the original AlphaZero target" — [Willemsen et al. 2021](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/30870/30870.pdf)
- Go-Exploit (Trudeau & Bowling 2023): policy improvement from search holds "as long as the value estimates used in search are sufficiently accurate under the current policy"; their archive-based start-state selection reduced value loss over visited states (Connect Four 0.196 -> 0.151; 9x9 Go 0.293 -> 0.241) and won 0.58-0.79 head-to-head vs AlphaZero at matched checkpoints — [Go-Exploit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12359)
- ReBeL and Student of Games train the value net on *search outputs* (root PBS value vector averaged over CFR iterations; GT-CFR bootstrapped counterfactual values) but in both cases the search output at a PBS is an equilibrium-solved value, not a 1-ply noisy maximum — [ReBeL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13544); [Student of Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03178)

### Inferences
- No source reports the specific observation "refitting the value head on search targets removed the search gain", so the following is inference: a 1-ply search's gain comes from the critic *ranking* 16 candidate successor states; refitting the critic to a different target (e.g., the search's own root value, or a target with different variance/horizon) changes its ranking noise and calibration, and any decrease in ranking precision cuts directly into the test-time search gain. MuZero's choices (value loss weight 0.25, TD-5 over 0-step search value, target network) and KataGo's (outcome main head, search values only as auxiliaries) are all consistent with "do not let the critic chase the search".
- The recommended split is: search teaches the policy (completed-Q / soft targets), returns teach the value (TD(n) or outcome with a target network); if search values are used for the critic at all, use them as low-weight auxiliary heads (KataGo) or as the DM-Control-style regression only when data is scarce.
- Because Polytopia's search gain is critic-driven, the critic's quality should be tracked as a first-class metric during any distillation run (e.g., AUC of critic ranking vs rollout outcome on held-out candidate sets), not just policy loss.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found of "value-head refit after search distillation" losing search gains; the closest quantitative evidence is MuZero Unplugged Table 8 (0-step vs TD-5) and Willemsen's bias argument.

## Q5. Imperfect-information search-based improvement: ReBeL, Player/Student of Games, DeepNash/R-NaD; Stratego/poker lessons for fog-of-war strategy games

### Takeaway
The sound approaches (ReBeL, Student of Games) make the network a function of the *public belief state* and distill equilibrium-solved subgame outputs (CFR average policy, counterfactual values), playing a random CFR iteration's policy at test time for safety; DeepNash avoided search altogether because Stratego's hidden information (10^66 deployments, 10^535 states) makes subgame solving intractable, and still reached 84% vs expert humans with a model-free regularized Nash dynamics. For fog-of-war 4X with little disambiguation, PIMC/determinized search is known to suffer strategy fusion and non-locality; recent AlphaZero-style work (MAPLE 2026) aggregates several sampled world states in one tree and gains +291 Elo (Phantom Go) / +136 Elo (Dark Hex) over a PIMC baseline, and update-equivalence search (Sokota et al. 2024) avoids subgame solving entirely when non-public information is large.

### Cited Findings
- ReBeL (Brown et al. 2020): a PBS is "a joint probability distribution over the agents' possible infostates"; values must be defined over PBSs because in imperfect-information games an action's value depends on how often it is chosen. The value network maps a PBS to a value per infostate; the policy network (poker only) is trained on the average policy from each solved subgame and used only to "warm start" CFR. Training target: "the value vector for the root infostates is added to the training dataset for v_hat". Test-time safety: "pick a random iteration and assume all players' policies match the policies on that iteration"; Theorem 3 bounds exploitability by roughly (delta C1 + delta C2 / sqrt(T)) with delta the value-net error. Results: +165 +-69 mbb/g vs Dong Kim (AIVAT), +45 +-5 vs Slumbot, +9 +-4 vs BabyTartanian8; Liar's Dice 1x4f exploitability 0.017 vs 0.001 for tabular CFR; depth-2 subgames with 1,024 CFR iterations in Liar's Dice; epsilon = 0.25 exploration in training, 0 at test. A value net trained on random PBSs "fails to learn anything valuable". No policy-only (no-search) ablation is reported — [ReBeL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13544)
- Student of Games (Schmid et al. 2021): GT-CFR grows a public tree with a regret-update phase (CFR+) and an expansion phase; the CVPN takes beta = (s_pub, r) and outputs counterfactual values per infostate for both players plus the acting player's policy. Value targets come from the game outcome (TD(1) on main-line states) and from bootstrapped GT-CFR solves at queried beliefs (recursive queries added with probability ~0.1-0.2); policy targets are the regret-update-phase policies for all infostates in the root public state; "sound self-play" uses a safe re-solving auxiliary game. Budgets: chess/Go training SoG(400,1), eval up to 60k sims; poker SoG(10, 0.01); Scotland Yard SoG(400,1). Results: chess SoG(60k,10) +420 vs AlphaZero +592 and Stockfish(16 threads, 4 s) +530; Go SoG(16k,10) +1970 vs Pachi(100k) +869; poker +7 +-3 mbb/h vs Slumbot (+434 +-9 vs LBR); Scotland Yard 55% vs PimBot at 10M sims. Theorem 2 bounds exploitability by (5D+2)(F epsilon + N U sqrt(A/T)) — [Student of Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03178)
- DeepNash (Perolat et al. 2022): learns "without doing any form of search or explicit opponent modelling" because "Stratego computationally challenges all existing search techniques as the search space becomes intractable" (10^535 states, 10^66 deployments) and search in imperfect information also requires estimating the opponent's private information. R-NaD: reward transformation by a KL penalty to a regularization policy (eta = 0.2), replicator/NeuRD dynamics to a fixed point (NeuRD beta = 2.0, v-trace rho_bar = c_bar = 1.0, lr 5e-5, batch 768 trajectories, target averaging gamma = 0.001), then the fixed point becomes the next regularization policy (10k steps per update for m <= 100, 100k for 100 < m <= 165, 35k after; 7.21M max steps; fine-tuning threshold eps = 0.03 with 32 probability quanta). Actions are factorized (select piece, then destination; "3600 possible actions at every step"); the input includes a hand-computed, policy-independent public-information tensor giving per-square piece-type probabilities under uniform sampling consistent with the move history. Results: 42/50 (84%) vs Gravon experts, 2022 rating 1799 (3rd); 97-100% vs all bots tested — [DeepNash](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15378)
- Update-equivalence framework (Sokota et al., ICLR 2024): subgame-solving search becomes impractical "when the amount of non-public information is large"; instead, search replicates the updates of last-iterate learning algorithms (mirror descent for cooperative, magnetic mirror descent for adversarial), which do not depend on public information; in Hanabi this matches or exceeds public-information search using two orders of magnitude less search time, with gains in 3x3 Abrupt Dark Hex and Phantom Tic-Tac-Toe — [Sokota et al. 2023/24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13138)
- PIMC analysis (Long, Sturtevant, Buro, Furtak, AAAI 2010): strategy fusion (separate per-world searches can favor different moves and the combination can be wrong for every world) and non-locality (a node's value depends on information outside its subtree); PIMC's gap to the equilibrium player is predicted by three measurable tree properties — leaf correlation (probability sibling terminals share a payoff), bias (probability the game favors one player), and disambiguation factor (how quickly information sets shrink with depth; d_f = 0 means "p never gains any direct knowledge of his opponent's private information", d_f = 1 collapses to perfect information). Trick-taking games disambiguate quickly; "in a game like poker, no private information is directly revealed until the game is over". The paper notes PIMC "could be substantially worse against a player that attempts to exploit its mistakes" — [Long et al. 2010](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/7562/7562-13-11092-1-2-20201228.pdf)
- MAPLE (Li, Guei, Wu, Wu; IEEE CoG 2026, arXiv 22 May 2026): PIMC determinization in AlphaZero-style training causes strategy fusion; IS-MCTS avoids it but is costly with neural nets; MAPLE aggregates policy and value evaluations from several sampled world states within one search tree, with a Siamese-network sampler to pick informative world states; +291 Elo on Phantom Go and +136 Elo on Dark Hex vs the PIMC-based AlphaZero baseline — [MAPLE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24139)

### Inferences
- Polytopia's fog has a low disambiguation factor (unexplored regions stay unknown for many turns) but, unlike poker, most of the hidden state is static terrain/cities rather than adversarial private choices; the strategy-fusion risk is concentrated in scouting and threat-assessment decisions where different determinizations disagree. The cheap mitigation used by MAPLE and (older) ISMCTS is to score each candidate turn against *several* sampled hidden states and aggregate (mean or CVaR), which simultaneously reduces critic noise — the primary problem — and removes the worst strategy-fusion errors.
- A full PBS value function (ReBeL/SoG) is unlikely to be tractable for a 4X map; DeepNash's public-information tensor (policy-independent belief features as network input) and the update-equivalence idea (search that reproduces a regularized-policy-update rather than a subgame solve) are the scalable precedents. The Gumbel completed-Q target is itself a regularized policy update (MPO-style), so distilling it is consistent with the update-equivalence view.
- None of these papers distill a *determinized 1-ply maximum* into the policy as-is; ReBeL/SoG distill equilibrium-solved subgame policies, DeepNash distills nothing. Distilling a determinized argmax is the configuration most exposed to strategy fusion; the soft, multi-determinization target is less exposed.

### Gaps
- No study found of search-based policy improvement in a fog-of-war 4X or RTS game with AlphaZero-style distillation; the closest are Phantom Go / Dark Hex (MAPLE), Scotland Yard (SoG), and Stratego (no search).
- ReBeL has no policy-only ablation, so the share of its strength attributable to the distilled policy network vs test-time search is unknown.

## Q6. Student-teacher label noise: weighting labels by search confidence/advantage; when imitating a near-identical teacher yields no gain

### Takeaway
The standard fix for "labels are noisy samples from a policy close to the student" is to weight each label by a monotone function of its estimated advantage and to zero-out labels with no measured advantage: CRR (f = 1[A>0] or exp(A/beta), A estimated against m = 4 policy samples, exp weight clipped at 20) beats behaviour cloning by large margins and MuZero Unplugged beats CRR; AWR uses exp(A/beta) with beta = 0.05 and weight cap 20; the Gumbel completed-Q target is the same idea with a bounded scale and "zero advantage for unvisited actions". KataGo additionally oversamples positions where the search target disagrees with the prior ("policy surprise weighting": half the sample weight proportional to KL(prior || search target)).

### Cited Findings
- CRR (Wang et al. 2020): policy objective argmax_pi E_{(s,a)~B}[f(Q,pi,s,a) log pi(a|s)] with f = 1[Â(s,a) > 0] (binary) or f = exp(Â(s,a)/beta) (exp, beta = 1, clipped at 20); advantages estimated against the policy's own samples, Â_mean = Q(s,a) - (1/m) sum_j Q(s,a^j) or Â_max = Q(s,a) - max_j Q(s,a^j), a^j ~ pi, m = 4; BC is the f = 1 special case and "is highly dependent on the quality of the dataset"; binary wins on low-complexity tasks (Cartpole Swingup 860 vs 664 exp vs 386 BC), exp on high-dimensional ones (Humanoid Corridor 918 exp vs 484 binary vs 220 BC); on humanoid-run binary keeps only ~10-12% of dataset actions, binary-max 2-5% — [CRR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15134)
- AWR (Peng et al. 2019): pi_{k+1} = argmax_pi E_{(s,a)~D}[log pi(a|s) exp((1/beta)(R_{s,a} - V(s)))], derived from maximizing expected improvement under a KL constraint to the sampling policy; beta = 0.05 for all experiments, weight clip omega_max = 20; value fit by TD(lambda), lambda = 0.95; removing the baseline "noticeably hampers performance"; the single-sample exponentiated advantage is a biased estimate but effective — [AWR](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00177)
- MuZero Unplugged offline Atari (46 games, median human-normalized): BC 53.3%, CRR 155.6%, MuZero Unplugged (search targets + MCTS acting) 265.3% — [MuZero Unplugged](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294)
- KataGo policy surprise weighting: among full-search moves, about half the total sample weight is uniform and the other half is distributed in proportion to the KL divergence from the policy prior to the search policy target; each position is written floor(w) times plus one more with probability w - floor(w); fast-search positions can get nonzero weight if their KL exceeds a threshold; described as "one of the larger improvements" between KataGo runs (no isolated Elo) — [KataGo methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)
- KataGo soft policy target ("policy optimism" family): an auxiliary head predicts the search target raised to 1/T with T = 4, weighted 8x the normal policy loss, which "greatly improves the speed of learning of the policy"; a separate "optimistic policy" head trained with weight clamp(0,1, sigmoid((z_value-1.5)*3) + sigmoid((z_score-1.5)*3)) where z = (short-term outcome - prediction)/predicted std, used for both sides in search at test time, "something like 40 to 90 Elo" — [KataGo methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)
- Gumbel completed-Q target gives "zero advantage to the unvisited actions", so unsearched actions are neither pushed up nor down; bounded scale c_scale caps the total-variation movement per update (Muesli Theorem 4.4.1: max TV = tanh(c/2); c = 1 used) — [thesis](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Self-distillation theory under label noise (Das & Sanghavi, ICML 2023): the student is trained on a mix of teacher predictions and given labels; in the high-noise regime the optimal weight on the teacher's predictions is "surprisingly greater than 1", and self-distillation "effectively reduces the variability of student predictions compared to the teacher's" — i.e., it acts as a regularizer, not a source of new information — [Das & Sanghavi 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13304)
- On-policy distillation in LLMs (Ding & Zhang, Aug 2026): teacher-provided per-token advantages contain "substantial noise whose prevalence increases with teacher scale", yet the student "converges to comparable performance regardless of whether noisy supervision is retained or removed"; the gains are attributed to suppressing low-log-probability tokens, "which requires no teacher" — [Ding & Zhang 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31046)
- Grill et al.: the Learn variant (distill pi_bar = argmax_y q^T y - lambda_N KL(pi_theta, y), lambda_N = c sqrt(sum_b n_b)/(|A| + sum_b n_b), closed form pi_bar = lambda_N pi_theta / (alpha - q)) gives "the main improvement" at N_sim = 5 on Ms Pacman; the two methods "start reaching comparable levels of performance when N_sim >= 24" — [Grill et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12509)

### Inferences
- The Polytopia symptom "search overrides the policy 87% of the time by margins smaller than its own score noise" means the realized advantages are mostly noise. Under CRR-binary filtering with Â_max against the policy's own samples, very few candidates would pass (CRR reports 2-12% pass rates even on real data), and under exp weighting with a small scale the target collapses toward pi — both correctly produce "little update", which is the honest answer when the teacher is noise. Zero gain is therefore expected unless (a) the per-candidate score noise is reduced (more determinizations/rollouts per candidate, Sequential Halving to concentrate evaluations on the top few) and/or (b) learning is concentrated on the subset of states where the search's margin is large relative to its noise (KataGo surprise weighting; CRR binary filter on a z-scored advantage).
- A direct diagnostic is to compute, per state, the z-score of the top candidate's margin against the empirical score std across re-evaluations, and to measure how much of the 104 Elo is produced by states with |z| > 1-2. If most of the gain comes from a minority of high-margin states, train only on those (and use the KL anchor only there).
- The Das & Sanghavi and Ding & Zhang results warn that any apparent gain from distilling a near-identical teacher may be a regularization effect (sharpening the student toward its own high-probability actions) rather than true policy improvement — worth separating with a control run that distills the policy's own unsearched samples.

### Gaps
- No paper explicitly analyses "imitating a teacher that equals the student plus zero-mean noise yields no gain"; the closest are the Gumbel counterexample (argmax over top-n without the Gumbel correction is not an improvement), Grill's discretization-noise argument, and the self-distillation theory papers (supervised setting).

## Q7. Cost-aware designs: search on a fraction of states, small-simulation-count targets, mixing policy-gradient and search targets

### Takeaway
KataGo's playout-cap randomization (full 600-playout search on only p = 0.25 of moves, 100 playouts elsewhere, policy targets recorded only on full-search moves) gave a 1.37x speedup; Gumbel's 16-simulation training is 11.3x cheaper per step than 200-simulation training on 9x9 Go while still learning; Grill et al.'s Learn variant (distill the regularized-optimization solution pi_bar) captured most of the gain at 5 simulations; Muesli reaches MuZero-level Atari performance with a clipped-MPO regularized policy-gradient target and no deep search, giving a non-search baseline for the same kind of bounded policy improvement.

### Cited Findings
- KataGo playout cap randomization: p = 0.25 of turns use a full search capped at N = 600 (later 1000) nodes; the rest use n = 100 (later 200) with Dirichlet noise and exploration disabled; only full-search turns are recorded for policy training; ablation speedup 1.37x; full-search-only runs at a fixed 600 playouts were "prone to early overfitting" and converged worse; combined KataGo techniques gave ~9.1x in ablations and ~50x less compute than ELF OpenGo overall — [KataGo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10565)
- MuZero Reanalyse: 80% of policy-target updates come from re-searching stored states rather than new environment interaction, i.e., search compute is spent on targets, not acting — [MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265); fraction/data trade-offs in [MuZero Unplugged Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294)
- Gumbel Table C.1 training-step speedups (9x9 Go, not learner-bound): n=32 5.9x, n=16 11.3x, n=8 16.2x, n=4 24.3x vs n=200 — [thesis](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Grill et al. variants: Act (act from pi_bar), Search (sample tree actions from pi_bar), Learn (distill pi_bar), All; at N_sim = 5 "the main improvement comes from using the policy optimization solution pi_bar as the learning target (Learn variant)"; at N_sim = 50 the gain is "mostly due to sampling according to pi_bar during search" — [Grill et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12509)
- Soemers et al. 2019 (Ludii-era board games, 1600 MCTS iterations): tree-search policy gradient with all-action sum over MCTS Q-values, with the softmax parametrized as z = (theta_ce + theta_tspg)^T phi with theta_ce frozen so that gradients do not vanish for low-probability actions; pi_tspg "consistently outperforms" the cross-entropy-to-visit-counts policy in strength, though the CE policy was more useful as a biased play-out policy inside MCTS (e.g., Hex 11x11 86.5% vs 71.0%) and pi_tspg has the lowest entropy — [Soemers et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05809)
- Muesli (Hessel et al. 2021; thesis ch. 4): a regularized policy-optimization agent using clipped MPO advantages with clipping threshold c = 1 (max total-variation distance tanh(c/2) between pi_prior and pi_CMPO, independent of the number of actions) "matches the state of the art on Atari, even without deep search" — [thesis ch. 4](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Go-Exploit: choosing self-play start states from an archive of previously visited/searched states (not always s_0, with probability lambda of starting at s_0) more than doubled trajectories per learning step (Connect Four 147 -> >323; 9x9 Go 75 -> >147) and improved head-to-head win rates vs AlphaZero at equal checkpoints (0.58-0.79) — [Go-Exploit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12359)
- MuZero Unplugged Table 4 shows CRR-style advantage-weighted BC (155.6 / 153.0) as the best non-search target and the Reanalyse (search) target as better (203.2 policy head, 239.9 value head, 265.3 MCTS) — [MuZero Unplugged](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294)

### Inferences
- The cheapest high-value design for Polytopia given a 16-candidate turn search: (1) run the search on a random fraction of turns (KataGo p ~ 0.25) and record search targets only there; on the rest act from the policy (or a 4-candidate fast search) and record only value/return targets; (2) on searched turns, replace the one-hot label with the completed-Q softmax over the 16 candidates with the Gumbel scale (c_visit = 50, c_scale tuned on normalized scores), and weight the sample by the KL between prior and target (surprise weighting) so that low-margin states contribute little; (3) spend the saved budget on noise reduction per candidate (2-4 determinizations/rollouts for the top 4-8 after a halving round) rather than more candidates; (4) keep the critic on return/TD targets with a target network and lower loss weight, and (5) keep a PPO/Muesli-style bounded policy-gradient term on the unsearched turns so the searched-turn KL term is an additive "search advantage" loss, which is the Grill/Muesli reading of search as regularized policy optimization.
- Re-search of stored states (Reanalyse) is attractive here because the engine is cheap and the candidates/determinizations can be regenerated offline for 23M decisions without new games; the policy-side gain in MuZero Unplugged came entirely from such reanalysed targets.

### Gaps
- No paper combines PPO's clipped objective with a Gumbel/visit-count KL term in a single actor-critic; the closest evidence is Grill (Learn variant inside MuZero), Soemers (TSPG vs CE), and Muesli (CMPO + model, no deep search). The combination is unvalidated in the literature as far as found.

## Q8. Synthesis: concrete recipes for the Polytopia turn-search setting (16 sampled whole turns, 1-ply opponent rollout, critic scoring, determinized hidden state)

### Takeaway
The literature points to one specific explanation of +0 Elo (hard labels of a noisy, near-identical teacher) and a specific repair (Gumbel completed-Q soft targets over the sampled candidates, noise reduction via Sequential Halving and multi-determinization, advantage/surprise weighting, critic kept on return targets), with cost-aware scheduling from KataGo/Reanalyse.

### Cited Findings
- Hard-label vs soft-label gaps: ExIt +50 +-13 Elo for TPT over CAT at identical top-1 accuracy — [ExIt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439); Gumbel L_simple < L_completed at every n — [thesis Fig. 5.3a/C.1](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)
- Target formula to implement: pi'(a) = softmax(logits(a) + (c_visit + max_b N(b)) * c_scale * q_norm(a)), unvisited a get q = v_mix = (v_hat + N * sum_visited pi q / sum_visited pi)/(N+1), q_norm min-max rescaled to [0,1]; defaults c_visit = 50, c_scale = 1.0 (Go/chess) or 0.1 on [-1,1]-normalized Q (Atari); loss KL(pi', pi_theta) — [thesis eqs. 5.8-5.12](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf); [mctx](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mctx)
- Sample-based form with proposal correction when candidates are not drawn from pi exactly: Î pi(a) = (beta_hat/beta)(a) exp(Q(s,a)/tau)/Z — [Sampled MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06303)
- Candidate budget: m = min(n,16); m = 4 learned slower; K = 2 insufficient in Atari, K = 3-5 sufficient in low-dim control, K = 50/362 near-full in Go — [thesis](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf); [Sampled MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06303)
- Sample weighting: KataGo surprise weighting (half of weight proportional to KL(prior || search target)) and CRR binary/exp advantage filters with m = 4 policy samples and clip 20 — [KataGo methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md); [CRR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15134)
- Critic targets: TD-5 with target network beat 0-step search-value regression (126.6% vs 115.3% median) and value-loss weight 0.25 in Reanalyse — [MuZero Unplugged](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294); [MuZero App. H](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265)
- Schedule: full search on p = 0.25 of moves, policy targets only there — [KataGo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10565); 80% of policy updates from reanalysed stored states — [MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265)
- Imperfect information: aggregate several sampled world states per candidate to blunt strategy fusion (+291/+136 Elo over PIMC in Phantom Go/Dark Hex) — [MAPLE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24139); policy-independent public-belief input features as in DeepNash — [DeepNash](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15378)

### Inferences
- Recipe A (minimal change, directly supported): keep the existing 16-candidate search; replace the CE-on-chosen-turn loss with KL(pi', pi_theta) where pi' is the Gumbel completed-Q softmax over the 16 candidate turns (sequence-level log-probs as logits), with scores z-normalized per state and c_scale tuned so that the expected per-state TV(pi', pi) is modest (Muesli's c = 1 corresponds to max TV ~0.46); keep the parent-KL anchor but reduce it, since pi' already contains the prior. Expected effect: recovers part of the search gain only where the critic margin is real; a control run with c_scale -> 0 (pure self-distillation) separates regularization effects from improvement.
- Recipe B (noise-aware): before labelling, run one or two Sequential Halving rounds on the 16 candidates with independent determinizations/rollouts (16 -> 8 -> 4, costing ~1.75x the current rollouts), set N(a) to the number of evaluations each survivor received so that the sigma scale grows legitimately, and compute surprise weight w = 0.5 + 0.5 * KL(pi || pi')/mean KL; train on searched turns with weight w. This targets the exact failure (labels below noise) and mirrors KataGo's two largest policy-side wins.
- Recipe C (cost-aware reanalyse): since the labels came from a fixed parent, regenerate pi' offline for the stored 23M decisions with the *current* student as the proposal and critic every few epochs (MuZero Reanalyse), on a 25-50% subset chosen by surprise; this converts a one-shot distillation into an iterated ExIt loop without new self-play.
- Critic handling: do not refit the value head on search values; keep TD(n)/outcome targets with a target network and a reduced loss weight; if a search-derived value signal is wanted, add it as a low-weight auxiliary head (KataGo short-term heads) and monitor candidate-ranking AUC of the critic on held-out searched states as the gating metric for the test-time search gain.
- Imperfect-information handling: evaluate each candidate under 2-3 sampled hidden-state determinizations and aggregate (mean, or mean minus k*std for risk) before forming pi'; this is the MAPLE/ISMCTS-lite step and also reduces critic noise.

### Gaps
- All recipes are syntheses; none has been validated on a fog-of-war 4X game in the literature found. The quantitative expectations (how much of the +104 Elo is recoverable) cannot be sourced.
- No source gives a principled rule for choosing c_scale against a known critic noise level; it has to be tuned (the Gumbel paper tuned it per domain: 1.0 for Go/chess, 0.1 for Atari on normalized Q).
