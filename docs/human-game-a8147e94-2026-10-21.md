One game against one human is a pattern to test, not a measured weakness.

## Why the policy plays this way

1. **Short credit horizon.** The reward is win/loss only, discounted per decision with gamma 0.995
   (`c5-omni-s1` protocol: `gamma .995`, `gae_lambda .99`, no turn clock). The bot takes ~15 decisions per turn in
   this game, so a turn is worth 0.995^15 ≈ 0.93: a payoff 10 turns away is worth about half, 20 turns away about a
   quarter. A lumber hut (3 stars now, growth over many turns) looks bad; clearing a forest (+1 star now) looks good.
   The horizon in turns shrinks further as the army grows and decisions per turn rise.
2. **A single lineage of opponents.** c5 trained 50% against its current self, 15% against its own last 8 snapshots,
   5% against frozen c3. Every opponent is the same warrior/catapult-pressure family, so early pressure wins before
   economy pays off and the policy never meets a player who turtles behind archers and out-grows it.
3. **Search inherits it.** The turn search scores positions with the same critic.

## Levers, and which ones to use

| Lever | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Turn clock (`turn_gamma` 1.0 or 0.99, `turn_lambda` .95, `advantage_floor` .01) | **Use** | Directly fixes cause 1. c6-credit: turn clock +8–10 pts over per-decision control. Already implemented. |
| Load the parent critic (`critic_path`) + short `critic_warmup` | **Use** | c6-credit's 1-h arms all lost to the parent mainly from the fresh-critic reset. Warm-up refits the parent critic to the new discount targets. |
| Style-biased opponents (new) | **Use** | Fixes cause 2 cheaply: frozen opponents with a fixed per-game logit bias on action families (boomer: + build/lumber hut/sawmill, − clear forest, − train; archer turtle: + Archery, + archers; rider rush; naval). Opponents only, so the learner stays on-policy. |
| Broader frozen pool across lineages | **Use** | c3, the ablation arms, the c6 arms and older c5 snapshots via `frozen`. No new code. |
| Mid-game start states (new) | **Use** | 20–25% of lane resets start from turn 6–12 positions reached by style-biased playouts, plus saved human-game snapshots. The learner then practises from positions its own play never reaches (behind on economy, facing archers). |
| "Golden trajectories" as a BC loss | **Don't** | A handful of human games would overfit and pull toward weaker play. Use them as start states and as an eval set instead. |
| A bigger fixed `entropy_coef` | **Don't** (keep .005) | Uniform per-step noise randomizes single moves inside a 15-decision turn (mostly blunders), not coherent plans. |
| Target-entropy control per decision type (new, from duper) | **Use** | Duper adapts each action head's entropy coefficient toward a target entropy (EMA, clamped) and anneals the target 0.5 → 0.35 → 0.25 → 0 over training. That keeps choices like which unit or building from collapsing early, which is the diversity pure self-play needs. |
| `unit_entropy_coef` (small, e.g. .002) | **Optional** | Already implemented; keeps the unit-type choice from collapsing. No prior run used it, so it adds risk to an 8-h run. |
| New map seeds | **Not a lever** | c5 already trains on `proposed-v1` (10 map types, seeds 200000–300000). More seeds of the same mix add no strategic diversity. |
| Potential shaping (`pbrs_coef`) | **Don't** | Tested in ablation-v1: hurt (37% vs c3). Its potential counts stars and units, so it literally rewards unit spam. An income/city-level potential is untested. |
| Exploiter learner (AlphaStar) | **Later** | The strongest diversity tool, but a second learner roughly doubles cost. |

## What duper's RL does (`duper/rl_ray_deploy`)

- **100% self-play.** Every seat is the training model (`player2model_version_management.py`); a second model
  appears only in evaluation. No historical pool, no league.
- **Turn clock.** gamma 1 / lambda .95 inside a turn and 0.95 / .95 only at the step that ends the player's turn
  (`time_decay`, `flow/utils.py` GAE). Ours today is 0.995 per decision (≈ 0.93 per turn).
- **Adaptive per-head entropy.** `flow/ppo.py`: per action head, coef += 1e-3 × (target − EMA entropy), clamped to
  [min, max]; targets annealed by train step (0.5 to 100k, 0.35 to 200k, 0.25 to 300k, then 0 with coef in
  [.01, .1]).
- **Sparse reward.** +10 for eliminating the opponent; an auxiliary "unnecessary command" head in the policy loss.
- **Scale.** 512 actors on 2 nodes, 4 trainer GPUs, batch 8,192, lr 3e-4, data reuse 2.

So a pure self-play hero run is a config change for us (`self_play_probability 1`, `historical_probability 0`,
`frozen ()`). Duper's lesson is that its diversity comes from the entropy controller and the turn clock, not from
the opponent pool. Pure self-play with our fixed .005 entropy and per-decision discount would most likely
reproduce this bot's narrow strategy at greater cost. Self-play alone also risks forgetting how to beat older
strategies; keep the c5 ladder at every snapshot (and optionally a 5% frozen c5 seat) as the guard.

## Proposed run

1. **Code (about a day):** style-biased opponent wrapper; mid-game start-state pool generated by biased playouts
   (and loaded from saved human snapshots); persist public-site games to a Modal Volume so human positions accumulate.
   Bitwise and unit tests for both, smoke on Modal.
2. **1-h pilot** of the full recipe from `c5-omni-s1-final` with `critic_path`. Gate: decisive-game rate vs the
   parent ≥ 50%, and the economy panel moving in the right direction.
3. **8-h run** if the pilot passes. Modal ≈ $22/h per arm → ≈ $180 nominal, $270–350 with the preemption budget
   (1.5–2×), plus evaluation; or the Curio cluster at zero marginal cost with explicit authorization.

Evaluation beyond the usual deterministic ladder vs c5 (read decisive games; mirror caps run 22–27%):

- **Economy panel** on ladder games: share of stars spent on buildings vs units, forests cleared, mean city level and
  income at turns 10/15/20.
- **Style gauntlet:** win rate vs each style-biased opponent (boomer, archer turtle, rider rush) for c5 and the new
  checkpoint.
- **Human positions:** play out from saved human-game snapshots, both seats, with search.

A useful zero-code check first: run the economy panel on the c6 turn-clock checkpoints vs c5 to see whether the turn
clock alone already moves the bot toward building.
