# Plato: a 3.2M-parameter transformer from scratch, then peace (2026-11-09)

**Result.** Plato is a hybrid convolution + transformer network (`transformer-v3-p2-h4`, 6 blocks × 128 channels,
3.2M parameters). It was trained from random weights by self-play PPO on the game's 2.17.3 rules (1v1, 14×14
classic) for 85,270 updates, about 25.6 billion samples, ~337 H100-hours and $1,497.84 including evaluations.

It beats every bot in this repository under both rule sets:

- **With peace treaties legal** (the game's rules): .892 against hero-1v1 and .972 against the champion. Its $1,000
  checkpoint had scored .330 against hero-1v1 under these rules.
- **With diplomacy disabled:** .853 against hero-1v1 and .984 against the champion.

Against a replica of the built-in Crazy AI it went 48–0. Wins come by elimination: with peace legal, 0–2.8% of
games reach the 200-round cap.

It is not yet runnable from this repository: the `transformer-v3` network builder is not part of it, so the weights
in [`weights/plato/`](../weights/plato) can be verified but not played here (see the [model card](../weights/plato/README.md)).

Every number below comes from a panel whose matches, seeds and decision rules were frozen (with a SHA-256) before
the checkpoint was evaluated. The full data is in [benchmarks/2026-11-09-plato.json](benchmarks/2026-11-09-plato.json).

## How the matches are played

- **Engine and settings:** engine-rs, the game's 2.17.3 rules, 14×14 classic maps the network never trained on, 1v1,
  resignation disabled.
- **Seat pairing:** each map is played twice with the seats swapped. A full match is 1,024 maps = 2,048 games; the
  exploiter matches are 256 maps = 512 games.
- **Policies:** raw policies with sampled actions and no search.
- **Score:** (wins + draws/2) / games for the first-named side. Ranges are 99% intervals clustered by map.
- **The round cap:** games that reach 200 rounds (a harness limit, not a game rule) count for the side ahead on
  score. The JSON also gives an elimination-only score that counts them as draws. The two never differ by more than
  .014 here.
- **The two rules:**
  - *Peace legal* means the game's own rules, with diplomacy and peace treaties.
  - *No peace* masks diplomacy out. Stages 1–3 trained only under no peace.

## Stage 4: teaching it peace

Stages 1–3 trained with diplomacy disabled. At $1,000 the bot beat hero-1v1 .766 without peace but only .330 with
it. An exploiter that offers peace treaties beat it about half the time. An earlier attempt elsewhere at learning peace
from pure self-play had collapsed: both sides agreed to peace and played out the clock. So stage 4 was designed against
collusion:

- **Separate rule sets.** Each GPU runs four game sets of 1,024 games, each set either peace-legal or no-peace for
  its life. Peace-legal sets were two of four, then three of four after the $1,250 check.
- **Opponents that don't collude.** In peace-legal sets, 60% of games are against fixed opponents: hero-1v1, the
  champion, the CNN control, three earlier Plato checkpoints and both exploiters. 10% are historical snapshots and
  30% are self-play.
- **Collusion monitors.** Every 50 updates the run measures capped self-play games, the share of steps played at
  peace, and late-round play. It warns at 10–25% and pauses the run at 30–50%.
- **Bigger batch.** The "b2" batch: 524,288 samples, 32,768 minibatch, learning rate ×√2. Stage 4 still cost more
  per sample than stage 3 (about $70 vs $54 per billion), because of the frozen-opponent calls and the extra checks.

What happened, including what did not work:

1. **Pilot 1 (8 H100, 541 updates): peace learned, but no-peace strength regressed.** With peace legal, its score
   against hero-1v1 went from .317 to .721 in 35 minutes. But against its own $1,000 parent with peace disabled it
   scored .417 [.395, .438]. That failed the preregistered gate (lower bound > .45), and the pilot stopped.
   - Snapshots showed the drop opened when the learning rate reached its 4.24e-4 peak (.481 → .435 → .423).
   - Strength against fixed opponents without peace was unchanged (champion .959 vs .955 for the parent).
   - So it was optimizer noise against a sharply annealed parent, not lost skill.
2. **Pilot 2 (440 updates, learning rate annealed back to 1.41e-4)** recovered half the gap: .460 [.438, .482].
   That still failed the same gate by .012. The miss was reviewed and accepted, and the main run started from this
   checkpoint with a lower peak learning rate (2.12e-4).
3. **4a (16 H100, 5,280 updates):** self-play with peace legal began to stall. 10–11% of self-play games reached the
   cap (warning level), and 17–19% of steps were played at peace, a plateau. Games against fixed opponents still
   ended in eliminations.
   - The $1,250 check passed every safeguard: no-peace vs the parent .498, hero-1v1 .851 with peace legal, Cloak .992.
4. **4b, first 23 minutes:** peace self-play was cut from .30 to .10 to reduce stalling. That cut was reverted in
   favour of a different fix: make not winning count as losing.
5. **4b with cap = loss for both sides (u79,400 → u85,270, 3 h):** a game reaching the round cap now ends −1 for
   both seats, instead of +1 for the side ahead on score. Peace self-play stayed at .30. Stalling fell steadily (last
   ten 50-update windows of each segment):

| Segment | Self-play games capped | All peace-legal games capped | Steps at peace | Diplomatic actions per game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pilot (score at cap) | 0.0% | 0.1% | 6.2% | 27.9 |
| 4a (score at cap) | 7.8% (peak 10.5%) | 3.3% | 17.6% | 16.7 |
| 4b, cap = loss for both | **4.3%** | **1.7%** | **9.6%** | 17.5 |

Right after the 4b restart, capped self-play briefly peaked at 24.9%. Every game had restarted at the same moment,
so the games that run to the cap all finished in the same window. It settled within ~100 updates and then fell
below the 4a level.

The full monitor series (232 points) is in the JSON. The change of rule is not an ablation: it came mid-stage,
together with more training and learning-rate annealing.

## Negative results

- **Early, the transformer was worse per dollar than the CNN.** At equal spend against the CNN control it scored
  .402 at $25, .393 at $50 and .455 at $100. It passed the control only later (.631 against the control's final at
  $300).
- **Through $1,000 it was weak and exploitable under the game's real rules.** It scored .330 against hero-1v1 with
  peace legal. A from-scratch exploiter beat the $300 checkpoint mostly through peace, and a one-hour fine-tune of
  the CNN control beat it with Cloak infiltration (.87). Both exploiters lose to Plato (.996 and 1.000). Plato
  itself has not faced a new exploiter.
- **No-peace progress in stage 4 is small.** Against its own $1,000 checkpoint without peace, Plato scores .528
  [.506, .549] after $500 more training. Most of stage 4's gain is under peace-legal rules. Both stage-4 pilots
  failed their no-peace gate.
- **It still talks peace.** It makes about 17 diplomatic actions per game. In the final training window, 4% of its
  self-play games with peace legal still reached the cap.
- **Against the champion with peace legal, Plato loses every capped game on score.** 57 of 2,048 games (2.8%)
  reach the cap, and Plato trails on score in all 57. Training now scores a capped game as a loss for both sides,
  so Plato has no reason to lead on score at the cap. By elimination alone it is 1,990–1 against the champion.
  Against hero-1v1 all 5 capped games were also lost on score.
