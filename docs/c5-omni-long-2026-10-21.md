# c5-omni-long: the omniscient-critic recipe for 6 hours, two seeds (2026-10-21)

Question: how strong does the recipe of [the ablation readout](ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md) get with six
hours instead of one, and is it reproducible across seeds? Ship candidate for the campaign of
[campaign-log-2026-10-19-20.md](campaign-log-2026-10-19-20.md). Ledger `runs/c5/budget.json` (cap $290).

## Setup

Exactly `abl-omni-2`: parent c3 u4854 (`/retained/checkpoints/distributed-final-v1/c3-maps-ctl-update-4854/final.npz`),
14×14 `proposed-v1` maps, `omniscient_critic` with 60 critic-only warm-up updates, c3's recipe otherwise (lr 2e-4,
entropy 0.005, 3 epochs, batch 262,144, minibatch 16,384, segment 32, γ 0.995, λ 0.99, 5% of games vs frozen c3,
15% vs the run's own 50-update snapshots), 1×H100 learner + 12 × 16-vCPU actor containers (16 processes × 24
games), TF32 convolutions + fp32 heads (`POLYTOPIA_MATMUL_PRECISION=DEFAULT POLYTOPIA_HEAD_PRECISION=HIGHEST`).
Changed: the seed (201 → `c5-omni-s1`, 202 → `c5-omni-s2`), 21,600 s of training instead of 3,600, and the
checkpoint cadence (`checkpoint_every` 10, `snapshot_every` 50, so every 50-update snapshot is kept; disk only).
Launched 22:29 and 22:33 PDT 2026-10-20, four minutes apart, from commit `0f7d7a5`.

Before launch the learner was made to survive a replaced container properly: it used to restart from the parent at
update 0 while its actors kept the dead tunnel's address. `Learner._resume` now restores weights, Adam state, the
critic, the update count, the training log, the snapshot pool and the clock from `latest.npz`; actors re-resolve the
learner's address from the registry before restarting a crashed process and stop only on a final registry stop or
15 min of silence (`test_learner_resumes_from_latest_checkpoint`, 10 tests passed on Modal). Neither learner was
preempted in the end; Modal replaced four actor containers (two per arm) in the first hour and they rejoined on
their own.

Both fleets were verified inside the containers 6 min after launch (16 of 16 worker processes at a full core each,
12 of 12 containers connected and compiled) and from the registry hourly.

### Pre-registered pass

- 6-h final ≥ 65% of all games vs c3 with CI lower bound > 58%: **both seeds pass** (77.3%, lower bound 72.7%).
- Seeds within 5 points of each other: **at 6 h yes** (77.3% vs 77.3%, and 68.0% vs 66.4% vs OMNI-1h); **at 3 h
  no by a hair** (66.8% vs 71.9%, 5.1 points, CIs overlapping widely). The seeds' training curves were within 2–3
  points at every window, and the 3-h snapshots are different update counts (1050 vs 1150, s2's learner ran 11%
  faster), so the 3-h gap reads as snapshot-to-snapshot noise rather than a seed effect.
- Strength roughly doubles the 1-h result on the odds scale: OMNI-1h was 54% of all games / 65% of decisive vs c3;
  the 6-h finals are 77% / 84–86%, and they beat OMNI-1h itself 66–68% (78–80% of decisive). Three hours gives
  67–72%; the second three hours add another 5–10 points. The UPGO sibling's 3-h final (70.7%) sits between the two
  plain 3-h snapshots, i.e. UPGO neither helps nor hurts at 3 h on these boards.
- Games got shorter as the bots got stronger (mean 40 turns for the finals vs 50 for OMNI-1h and 52 for c3's own
  continuation) and the cap rate vs c3 fell to 8–10% of games (20–25 of 256).

## Search on the stronger final: leak-free d=1 on `c5-omni-s1-final` (seeds 388000–388099 × 2 seats)

The two finals tied vs c3 (77.3% each); s1 was taken as the winner on the combined boards (68.0% vs 66.4% against
OMNI-1h, fewer cap games vs c3). `experiments/modal_test_time_search.py::sweep`, tag `c5-search-v1`: lanes 16, depth
1, temperature 1.0, rollout temperature 1.0, determinized (leak-free), `proposed-v1` 14×14, opponents raw and greedy;
scorer the winner's own value head (the stale c3 head on the moved trunk) and, as a check, c3's head
(`value=`). 04:52–05:17 PDT on 16 × 32-core containers, $7.87 (`runs/c5/search/c5-search-v1/`,
`scripts/analyze_c5_search.py`).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| s1-final + leak-free d=1 | raw s1-final (mirror) | own head | 103–57–40 | 0.615 [0.57, 0.66] | 64% | **+81 [+51, +115]** | 4.4 | 0.88 |

Paired on the same maps and seats (Elo, 4,000-resample map bootstrap):

- search gain on the winner vs raw c3: **+83 [+21, +153]** with its own head, **+100 [+37, +173]** with c3's head;
- search gain in the mirror: **+81 [+51, +115]**;
- c3's head minus own head: +16 [−62, +100] (no scorer effect, as in the OMNI-1h study);
- for comparison, on the same kind of maps the same search gave c3 +70 [+31, +111] and OMNI-1h +85 [+28, +143] vs
  raw c3, +139 in the mirror.

So the search gain does not shrink as the policy gets stronger: a one-ply leak-free search is still worth about
+80–100 Elo on top of a bot that is already +255 over c3, and the two levers remain additive (raw +255, with search
+338–354 vs c3, at 4.4–4.8 s per searched turn on one 32-core container). The greedy lane's value estimate
correlates 0.62 with the outcome under the own head (0.51 under c3's head), i.e. the moved trunk did not degrade the
stale head's ranking. Note the Elo scale saturates here: 0.885 of all games is near the point where cap games
(6% of games) dominate the losses.

Per map type (score of the arm's games): the raw winner is weakest on drylands (0.69) and pangea (0.70) and search
lifts every type to 0.75–0.98; the mirror gain is largest on jungle (0.71) and classic (0.67), smallest on drylands
(0.50, 8 games) and highlands (0.54).

## Reading

1. **The recipe scales with time and is reproducible.** Six hours of the omniscient critic from c3 give a bot that
   beats c3 in 77% of all games (84–86% of decisive) deterministically on fresh maps, two seeds within 0.1 point of
   each other at 6 h and within 2–3 points along the whole training curve. The 1-h result (54%) was the start of a
   curve, not the end: 67–72% at 3 h, 77% at 6 h, and the sampled training record (82–87% of decisive in the last
   hour) was still rising at the end.
2. **The 6-h finals beat the 1-h checkpoint 66–68%** (78–80% of decisive) and the 3-h snapshots 58% — each tripling
   of compute so far has added roughly +60–100 Elo against the previous checkpoint.
3. **Search still adds +80–100 Elo on top**, with either scorer, so the ship configuration is `c5-omni-s1-final`
   raw at +255 vs c3 or with leak-free d=1 search at +338–354 (≈ 4.5 s per searched turn on 32 cores).
4. **UPGO did nothing at 3 h** on these boards (70.7% vs 66.8–71.9% for the plain 3-h snapshots; sibling report
   `c5-upgo`).
5. The bots play faster games as they improve (mean 40 turns vs c3's 52) with fewer cap games (8–10%), and recruit
   Catapults, Cloaks and Defenders as often as Warriors (Warrior share 25% vs c3's 43%).
