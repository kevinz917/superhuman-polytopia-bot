# 18×18 1v1 self-play from c3 (2026-10-19/20)

**Result: no stronger 18×18 bot.** The engineering is done and validated: 18×18 boards as ruleset
`bardur-1v1-v2`, a grown c3 that plays exactly like c3, and the size made a config value end to end. Three
self-play runs from grown c3 ($235 of ~$270 spent; cap $300) did not produce a checkpoint that beats its parent when
both play deterministically. The best (run 3, update 300) wins 47% of decisive deterministic games against it
and 60% when both sides sample, as in training (details below). **Grown c3 remains the recommended 18×18 policy**; the run-3 checkpoint is archived
for reference and as a warm start.

Final evaluation of the selected checkpoint (run 3 update 300) on untouched development seeds, frozen engine,
both seats (`docs/rl-18x18/l18-final-v3u300*.json`):

| Match | Seeds | W–L–cap | Win rate [95% CI] | Of decisive games |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vs grown c3, 18×18 `proposed-v1`, deterministic | 371000+128 | 93–105–58 | 36% [32, 41] | 47% |
| vs grown c3, 18×18, all 10 types × 24 maps, deterministic | 373000+100k | 172–197–111 | 36% | 47% |
| vs grown c3, 18×18 `proposed-v1`, both sampled (training's pairing) | 371200+64 | 51–34–43 | 40% [33, 47] | 60% |
| vs c3, 14×14 `proposed-v1`, deterministic | 371500+128 | 85–115–56 | 33% [29, 38] | 43% |

Targets were ≥65% vs grown c3 on 18×18 and a turn-cap rate ≤ ~20%. Neither was met: deterministic play is at
or slightly below the parent, the turn-cap rate in self-play stays at 31%, and on 14×14 the checkpoint is
behind c3 (43% of decisive games), so it is not a drop-in replacement there either.

The baseline finding behind the "tech-limited" premise also needs a correction: c3 is not tier-3 starved.
On 14×14 it researches ~13 of 26 technologies and 3.4 tier-3 techs per game; what it rarely reaches are the
tier-3 economy techs (Construction 18% of games, Trade 16%, Philosophy 12%, Chivalry 11%).

`parameter_embedding` did not need the (x, y) remap that was planned: its rows index parameter ids (unit,
structure, tech; < 128), not tiles. Move/attack targets use the conv features, which do not depend on the board
size. `target_logits` now reads only the first 128 rows, so one network runs on any board, and old checkpoints
give identical legal log-probs. The two new residual blocks are pre-activation (`x + conv(act(conv(x)))`) with a
zero second kernel, because the existing blocks' post-activation `silu(layer_norm(·))` is not the identity.

Games run 57% longer (88 vs 56 turns), and 31% of them reach the 150-round cap (14×14: 24% at 100 rounds).

Throughput at 18×18: actors 2.0–3.4k samples/s per 32-core container (14×14 ~5k). The learner trains
39k sample-epochs/s per H100 (the 16,384 minibatch on one H100 needs 45 GiB and OOMs; 4,096 per device fits).
4×H100 therefore takes ~52k samples/s at 3 epochs, about 19 actor containers' worth.

## The three runs

All: parent grown c3, `proposed-v1` map mix at 18×18, 4×H100 learner, 16–22 actor containers, 262,144-sample
batches, 3 epochs, critic bootstrap at the turn cap ("ctl"), no outside bots (pool = the run's own snapshots
plus frozen grown c3). Win rates vs grown c3:

| Updates | run 1 (sampled, training) | run 2 | run 3 | c3 campaign on 14×14 vs its parent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–99 | 50% | 43% | 49% | 57% |
| 100–199 | 46% | 49% | 54% | 65% |
| 200–299 | 31% | 52% | 58% | 68% |
| 300–399 | 16% | 53% | 60% | 69% |
| 400–499 | 17% | 47% | 59% | 73% |
| 500–599 | 13% | 41% | 56% | 70% |
| 600–699 | — | 37% | 53% | 72% |

(Share of decisive training games against the frozen parent; both sides sample. Equal policies read 42–50%
in the first windows, so ±5 points is noise.)

**Run 1** (c3's settings: lr 2e-4, entropy 0.005, γ 0.995, 5% frozen parent): entropy rose from 1.1 to 3.8 and
the policy collapsed within 300 updates (16% vs its parent). Stopped after 1 h. Cause: at 18×18 the raw
advantage std is 0.024–0.04 (14×14: 0.047–0.065), below the normalizer's floor of 0.05, which halved the policy
gradient. The loss was −0.018 ≈ −0.005 × entropy, so training was almost pure entropy maximization.

**Run 2** (advantage floor 0.01, entropy 0.002): no early collapse (54% of decisive games at u220), then a
slow slide to 37%. Stopped after 1.5 h. The deeper cause is the horizon. Self-play games at 18×18 have ~1,500
decisions per seat against ~785 on 14×14, so with a per-decision γ of 0.995 the outcome reaches the opening at
weight 0.995^1500 ≈ 5e-4 (14×14: 0.02). The critic stayed flat (value std 0.15 vs 0.28 on 14×14) and the
advantages it produced were mostly noise.

**Run 3** (γ 0.9974, the 14×14 horizon rescaled to 18×18 game length; 40-update critic-only warm-up; lr 1e-4;
entropy 0.002; advantage floor 0.01; 15% frozen parent, 10% own snapshots): the critic recovered (value std
0.27) and sampled play against the parent improved from 49% to 60% by update 300–400. It then drifted back to
53%. Deterministic play never moved past parity. Stopped at update 646 (1.0 h), when the trend had turned and
another hour had little chance of reaching 65%.

So larger boards are not a drop-in extension for this PPO setup. With the outcome ~2× further away in
decisions, the sparse ±1 reward drowns, and what helped (γ rescaled to the game length, critic warm-up, a
smaller step) bought a +10-point sampled gain, not the 65% target.

## Next steps (not done)

- Turn-level credit: per-turn γ (discount once per end-turn rather than per atomic action) or a value target
  with n-step returns over turns would give 18×18 the same horizon as 14×14 without hand-tuning γ.
- KL anchor to the parent (`ppo.loss` already supports `anchor_coef`) to stop the drift seen in all three runs;
  it costs one extra reference forward per minibatch (~25% learner throughput).
- Curriculum: mix 14×14 games into training (needs per-size batches on the learner, since view widths differ).
- More learner per actor: 18×18 is learner-bound at 4×H100; bf16 convs would roughly double learner throughput,
  but need the same rounding-path checks as the SFT trainer.
