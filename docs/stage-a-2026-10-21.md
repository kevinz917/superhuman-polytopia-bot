# Stage A: exact actor-side throughput fixes A1–A4, measured (2026-10-21)

Implements the four actor-side levers of [10x-training-plan-2026-10-21.md](10x-training-plan-2026-10-21.md)
("Stage A") that [throughput-research-2026-10-21.md](throughput-research-2026-10-21.md) identified as duplicated
work on the actors, then measures them: bitwise tests, the per-phase tick on one core, the production-shape tick on
a 16-process container, and a 1-hour run of the c5 recipe on the c5 fleet. Branch `claude/stage-a-actor-fixes-87824d`
(commit `ac3a6dc` + this report); ledger `runs/stage-a/budget.json` (cap $40), copied to `docs/stage-a/`.

## What changed

| Lever | Change | Where | Exact? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Learner-side values.** Actors run no critic. Rows carry both packed int16 views as before plus `(seg, pos)`; each 32-decision segment ships one entry of a per-segment table: the boundary's views (the next decision of the same seat at a cut, the final state's views of both seats at a truncation, zeros at a native ending), the terminal flag, the outcome and the shaping potential after the last decision. The learner scores every row and every boundary with its *current* critic (one chunked forward over the assembled batch, `shard_map` over devices) and runs `segment_gae`, a vectorized GAE over `[segments × 32]` that equals `gae()` per segment (turn clock, PBRS and UPGO included). The host→device copy of the views now happens in the assembler thread (overlapped with training); the training batch is gathered from that device copy | `distributed.py`: `SegmentCollector`, `segment_gae`, `Learner._assemble/_score/_device_batch`; `cpu_engine.Tick.final_views` | No: values come from the current critic, not the actor's ≤ 10-update-stale copy. Tested: learner-side GAE == actor-side GAE for the same values |
| A2 | **Trusted-action step.** `fast_engine.trusted_step`: `core.step`'s transition and bookkeeping (version, episode-done status, action count, truncation) without the `valid = … \| build_mask(state)[dst, param] \| …` recompute of every legal mask. `CpuGames(trusted=True)` selects it per tick with `tick(check=True)` running the full `core.step` on demand; actors check every `trusted_check_every` = 64 ticks and still raise on `status != 0` | `fast_engine.py` §7, `cpu_engine.py` | Yes, for actions drawn from the state's legal masks (bitwise test) |
| A3 | **Candidate-tile build mask.** `fast_engine.build_mask_rows`: a build-mask row can be nonzero only for an economic tile (own territory, no foreign unit) or, in the road column, a neutral tile. Economic tiles are gathered into the smallest bucket of 16/32/64 that fits (else whole board), their rows formed from `[k × tiles] @ [tiles × 128]` products and the own cities' structure inventory (bucket of 16 cities, else whole-board fallback), scattered into zeros; the road column is a per-tile vector; the bridge condition is applied last | `fast_engine.py` §6, `cpu_engine.py` prepare | Yes (bitwise test in every bucket, incl. the fallbacks) |
| A4 | **Pool rival per process.** A process draws its mode when it (re)draws its opponent: self-play for all lanes with the self-play probability, else one pool opponent for all lanes (random learner seat per lane); redrawn every 4 × games finished games as before. The time-averaged share of lanes per opponent is unchanged; the rival forward is one ~12-lane batch on rival processes and absent elsewhere, instead of a 4-lane bucket every tick everywhere | `distributed.py` actor loop (`draw_mode`) | Yes (distribution over opponents unchanged) |

Config knobs (`LearnerConfig`, all default on; set to false to get the previous behaviour): `learner_values`, `trusted_step`,
`trusted_check_every` (64; 0 = never), `build_rows`, `pool_per_process`. Evaluation (`fast=False`), `rl.search` and the
public engine interface are untouched; `gpu/` is unchanged.

Not done: A5's 1-epoch arm (a recipe change; `epochs` is already a knob) and A6 (ingestion worker processes). A5's
overlapped host→device copy is a side effect of A1's design (the put happens in the assembler thread).

## 1-hour run of the c5 recipe (`stage-a-1h`)

Launched 15:11 PDT (`modal run --detach … ::launch --run-id stage-a-1h`, app `ap-9JVjwAoq9KbwUaFZUdM5LR`); config
`docs/stage-a/config-stage-a-1h.json` = the `c5-omni-s1` recipe (`docs/c5-omni-long/budget.json`) from the c3 parent
with seed 203 and the Stage A knobs on; fleet 1×H100 learner (16 vCPU) + 12 × 16-vCPU actors (16 processes × 24
games, 24 GiB), TF32 trunk / fp32 heads as c5; 3,600 s of learner clock. Learner registered at 15:14, first batch at
+124 s, 192 connections at update 2 (compile 95–105 s per process). 14 containers on the app at 15:39 (no duplicate).

**Throughput.** 455 updates, 119M samples in 3,550 s of learner clock: **37.3k samples/s** against c5-omni-s1's 27k
(1.38×) and its 340 updates in the first hour. The learner was the wall from the first minute, as the research doc
predicted for 3 epochs: `inbox` 128/128 (backpressure) at every update, `ready_wait` ≈ 0, **6.7 s per update** (median;
`train_seconds` 6.7, c5: 5.4 s), `mean_lag` 2.8 updates, up to 15k stale rows dropped on single updates. The actor
fleet reported 38.4k steps/s while throttled; unthrottled it delivers 6.4k steps/s per 16-process container (the
profile above) ≈ 77k steps/s ≈ 65k learner samples/s for the 12 containers. The update got slower than c5's 5.4 s
because the value pass (A1: one critic forward with the view decode over ~280k rows, in the assembler thread) shares
the GPU with the three epochs; the host→device copy it absorbed was 1.1 s. So the run shows the actor gain only as
far as the learner absorbs it; the samples/s target of ≥ 2× (54k) is not met on this recipe, and needs A5 (1 epoch:
the learner's 4.4 s of epochs become ~1.5 s) or a cheaper value pass (decode each view once and reuse the features
for training, which also halves the per-epoch decode).

**Training-time record vs c3 at matched updates** (sampled, 5% of games, both seats; decisive share W/(W+L), ~500–900
decisive games per window; binomial ±4 pts):

Within noise of both c5 seeds at every window: the learner-side values, the trusted step, the candidate-tile masks and
the per-process rival do not change what the recipe learns per update. Entropy 2.0–2.4, critic std 0.27–0.31, warrior

**Incident (known: [modal-orchestrator-replay](c6-credit/ctl-2026-10-21.md)).** At 16:01 PDT (learner clock 2,809 s,
update 380) Modal replayed the run's `orchestrate` container: a second learner (`r437.modal.host`, registered
16:02:13, 824 s of life) and a second 12-container fleet came up on the same run id, the duplicate learner resumed
from `latest.npz` (update 380) and the original's update 381 waited 191 s while its actors re-resolved the address

at 3,550 s and wrote `training.json` (the file is one monotone history with the 191 s row; the two learners' rows
after update 380 cannot be told apart in the volume's `final.npz`, so the last 75 updates' record above is read with
that caveat; both trained the same recipe from the same checkpoint). The duplicate cost ≈ $4. Not spotted live: the
20-minute check at 15:39 saw 14 containers; the next look was at the end. The fix the memory note asks for (an
idempotent `orchestrate`) is still not in.

## Addendum: the 1-epoch arm (`stage-a-e1`, 40 min, approved after the run above)

Same recipe, fleet and code with `epochs: 1`, seed 204, 2,400 s of learner clock (app `ap-4KZdWBOYa6yQ4GB1KwsemP`,
launched 16:25 PDT; config `docs/stage-a-e1/config.json`, ledger `docs/stage-a-e1/budget.json`, cap $20). 14 containers
throughout, one learner in the registry, no orchestrator replay this time.

**367 updates, 96M samples in 2,405 s: 40.0k samples/s** (42–43k after the 130 s first batch; c5-omni-s1 27k,
`stage-a-1h` 37.3k). The GPU stopped being the wall (`train_seconds` 1.7 s, from 6.7 s at 3 epochs), but the update
still took 5.4 s on average because the main loop waited 3.7 s per update for the assembler thread
(`ready_wait_seconds` mean 3.7, max 5.8; `inbox` 125/128 full, so the receiver threads were not the limit either;
`received_samples_per_second` peaked at 64.5k). The learner's wall is now the host-side batch assembly: concatenating
~280k rows of 30.6 KB (8.5 GB) from the chunks, the stale-filter copies, the 8.5 GB host→device put from pageable
memory and the value pass, all on one Python thread contending with 192 receiver threads. The actor fleet was still
throttled (45.6k steps/s reported).

| updates (≈ samples) | `stage-a-e1` (1 epoch, 40k/s) | `stage-a-1h` (3 epochs, 37k/s) | c5-omni-s1 | c5-omni-s2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1–100 (26M) | 47% (384-425-144) | 48% | 52% | 49% |
| 101–200 (52M) | 58% (364-265-167) | 59% | 58% | 58% |
| 201–300 (79M) | 62% (279-170-110) | 61% | 64% | 62% |
| 301–367 (96M) | 63% (357-214-131) | 66% (301–400) | 67% (301–400) | 66% (301–400) |

At matched samples the 1-epoch arm is within noise of the 3-epoch runs through 79M samples and 3–4 points under at
96M (±3.6 pts binomial on 571 decisive games): consistent with the earlier 1-h ablation that found 1 epoch neutral,
and too short to say more. Spend ≈ $12.

**What the two runs say together.** The actor side delivers 2.2× per core (bench) and the fleet runs ≥ 45k steps/s
even throttled; the learner now limits at ~40k samples/s in its host assembly path, not the GPU. The next lever is
therefore A6-shaped: assemble the batch off the main process (worker processes decompress into shared-memory arrays
laid out as the final batch, so no concatenation), pin the host buffers for the device copy, and keep the value pass
on the device. With that the learner at 1 epoch should absorb the fleet's ~65k and the fleet, not the learner,
becomes the wall again at the actors' 2.2×.

## What is next

1. **Batch assembly off the main thread (A6, reshaped)**: the 1-epoch arm shows the host assembly path (concatenate +
   stale filter + 8.5 GB put + value pass on one thread) is the wall at ~40k samples/s. Worker processes writing
   decompressed rows straight into shared-memory arrays with the batch's final layout, pinned host buffers for the
   put, and the value pass kept on the device.
2. **Value pass cost** at 3 epochs: decode the views once per batch on the device and feed the features to both the
   value pass and the epochs (today each epoch decodes every minibatch again). Returns the 3-epoch update to ≤ 5.4 s.
3. **A5 as a recipe**: 1 epoch was within noise at matched samples over 40 min; the 6-h check (C2) remains before adopting it.
4. Make `orchestrate` idempotent (refuse when the registry entry is live) before the next long run; the replay hit
   `stage-a-1h` (third time in two days) but not `stage-a-e1`.
