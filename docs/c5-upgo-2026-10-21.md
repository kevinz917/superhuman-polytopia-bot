# c5-upgo: UPGO on top of the omniscient-critic recipe (2026-10-21)

Line C of the overnight campaign ([campaign log](campaign-log-2026-10-19-20.md), $75 of the $500 cap; ledger
`runs/c5-upgo/budget.json`). Question: does AlphaStar's upgoing policy update (UPGO) add strength on top of the
omniscient critic, the one change that beat c3 in the ablation ([readout](ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md))?

## What UPGO is here

AlphaStar (Vinyals et al. 2019, Methods) trained its policy with V-trace plus an "upgoing policy update": a second
policy-gradient term whose return target follows the actual trajectory while the taken action did at least as well
as the critic expected and bootstraps from the critic otherwise,

    G^U_t = r_t + ( G^U_{t+1}  if  Q(s_{t+1}, a_{t+1}) >= V(s_{t+1})  else  V(s_{t+1}) ),   Q(s_{t+1}, a_{t+1}) ~ r_{t+1} + V(s_{t+2}),

with the term (G^U_t - V(s_t)) weighted by a clipped importance ratio. Its RL ablation on StarCraft II reads
V-trace 49% -> +TD(lambda) 73% -> +UPGO 82% best-response win rate; UPGO is the one trick aimed at sparse,
long-horizon credit that this project had not tried.

Implementation on this branch (`src/polytopia_bot/rl/distributed.py`, `rl/ppo.py`):

- `upgo_returns(values, bootstrap, last_reward, terminal, gamma, end_turn, phi, phi_next)` computes G^U on the
  actor's seat sequences with the same prelude as `gae` (outcome on the last decision, per-decision or turn-clock
  discount, optional potential shaping). The last decision of a segment bootstraps the critic value of the next
  decision state, so a 32-decision segment cut behaves like a truncation. It also returns a per-decision `follow`
  flag: G^U_t continued the trajectory rather than bootstrapping V(s_{t+1}).
- `SeatTrajectories(upgo=True)` adds `upgo_advantage = G^U - V` and `upgo_follow` to every emitted row. V is the
  row's `value`, which under `omniscient_critic` is the omniscient critic's value (the actor runs the critic on both
  seats' views; the policy's own value head is unused), as the pre-registration required.
- The learner normalizes `upgo_advantage` over the batch exactly like the GAE advantage (centered, divided by
  max(std, advantage_floor)) and logs `raw_upgo_std`, `upgo_follow_fraction`, `upgo_positive_fraction`,
  `upgo_gae_corr`. `ppo.loss(..., upgo_coef)` adds `upgo_coef * -mean(min(r u, clip(r) u))` with the same PPO
  ratio r and clip as the GAE surrogate (PPO's clipped ratio stands in for AlphaStar's clipped importance weight).
- `upgo_coef` defaults to 0: rows carry no UPGO fields and the loss is the same expression as before (unit test
  `test_upgo_loss_is_off_by_default_and_adds_the_clipped_surrogate` checks bitwise equality of the loss value with
  and without the field at coefficient 0, and the hand-built 3-decision trajectories in
  `test_upgo_returns_follow_the_trajectory_only_while_it_beats_the_critic` check the formula for a win, a loss,
  the per-decision discount, the turn clock and a truncation). Tests ran on Modal: `tests/test_distributed_rl.py`
  11 passed, `tests/test_structured_repair.py` 16 passed.

Weight: `upgo_coef = 1.0`, as pre-registered. Both advantages are unit-scale after normalization, so the UPGO
term doubles the policy-gradient weight against the fixed entropy bonus (visible in the curve below); AlphaStar
also used equal weights, with unnormalized V-trace and UPGO terms.

So about half of the decisions bootstrap from the critic one step ahead and half carry the trajectory's own
return; the UPGO advantage has less than half the spread of the GAE advantage (lambda .99 over ~800 decisions per
game carries far more of the outcome than the one-step-or-follow rule) and only a .41 correlation with it, so the
two surrogates do push in measurably different directions. After batch normalization both have unit scale, which
is where the entropy dip comes from.

## Verdict

**UPGO is neutral on top of the omniscient critic at this scale.** Three hours of omni + UPGO reach the same
strength as three hours of omni alone: identical decisive win rate vs c3 (78.4 vs 78.8%), parity head to head
(+10 Elo, CI [-33, +52]), paired per-board difference +1.2 points [-3.6, +5.9], and training curves that overlay
within noise (63.8 vs 64.8% of decisive over the first 2 h, 75-80% in the last hour for both). The one visible
effect is on game length: UPGO's policy plays ~6 turns shorter against c3 and caps 10% of games instead of 15%,
which lifts its all-games win rate by 3.9 points, under the pre-registered +5. One seed each, so a 2-4 point effect
in either direction is not excluded; a >= 5 point effect on these boards is unlikely.

Why it does not help here, as far as the statistics say: the UPGO advantage has less than half the spread of the
GAE advantage (.020 vs .044 raw std) and a .41 correlation with it, so it is a different and much weaker signal; with
lambda .99 over ~800 decisions per game, GAE already carries most of the terminal outcome back along the trajectory,
which is the job UPGO was built to do under V-trace's heavy truncation in AlphaStar. With an omniscient critic the
one-step bootstrap that UPGO falls back to is also already good. The sparse +-1 reward does not change this: half
the decisions "follow" (the critic's value rose after the action) and half bootstrap, in both wins and losses.

What it cost besides the arm: the second unit-scale surrogate doubles the policy-gradient weight against the
entropy bonus and dipped entropy to 1.6 in hour 1 (recovered to 2.4 by hour 3); a run that keeps UPGO should halve
the learning rate or raise the entropy coefficient to compensate, or weight UPGO by its raw scale (coef ~.45) instead
of normalizing it to unit std.

Recommendation: do not keep UPGO in the recipe (no gain, one more knob, a lower-entropy first hour). The code stays
behind `upgo_coef` (default 0, byte-identical loss) for the 18x18 turn-clock setting, where the per-decision horizon is
twice as long and credit assignment was the diagnosed bottleneck, if that line wants a cheap second try. Strength
work goes to the levers that did move: longer omniscient-critic training (the sibling's 6-h finals) and search on
top of it.

Loose ends: actor processes exit on a connection reset during the first hello and the container returns without
restarting them (one of 12 containers lost at launch; replaced by hand with `more_actors`). The hello should get the
same reconnect loop as the data exchange. The arena `select` finished 128 x 2 games in 9 min on a 32-core
container, so the 45-min reservation per arena is ~5x conservative.
