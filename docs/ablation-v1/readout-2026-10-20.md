## Stage 0 — diagnosis of the 18×18 plateau (side session, branch `claude/diag-18x18-roundrobin-bb5639`)

Round-robin of grown c3 and four plateaued children, 10 pairs × 128 games on fresh 18×18 maps, sampled play:
every pair 44–56%, one transitive Bradley–Terry ordering (χ² 2.1 on 6 df), no 3-cycle; the three later children
sit +24–28 Elo (±19) over grown c3, run 2 at exact parity. **Verdict: no learning signal, not cycling.** A
PFSP pool is not indicated. Full table in that branch's `docs/ablation-v1/diagnosis-18x18-2026-10-20.md`.
Billed $14.4 (six Modal preemptions replayed matches).

## Stage 1 — precision gate (side session, branch `claude/precision-gate-d7f0e8`)

On 20,480 real actor rows per board, TF32 convolutions with fp32 heads give epoch-0 importance ratios with
mean k3 1.8e-7 and max |log r| 6e-3 (gate: 1e-4 and 0.02), about the size of the float16 weight transport the
runs already have. Learner epoch 1.92× faster at 14×14, 3.28× at 18×18. Setting used for every arm:
`POLYTOPIA_MATMUL_PRECISION=DEFAULT POLYTOPIA_HEAD_PRECISION=HIGHEST` on the learner. Actor fleet: 16-vCPU
containers (see that branch's `docs/ablation-v1/precision-gate-2026-10-20.md`). Billed ≈ $2.

## Stage 2 — five arms from c3 on 14×14, 1 h each

Setup as pre-registered: parent c3 u4854, `proposed-v1` maps, 1×H100 + 12 × 16-vCPU actors per arm, c3's recipe
as control, one change per arm. Modal preempted the learner containers of the omniscient-critic and PBRS arms
at ~13:40; both were relaunched unchanged at 13:50 (the learner now survives a restarted container).

Per map type the omniscient-critic arm beat c3 on every one of the ten (ridge 12–1, continents 16–5, archipelago

view and run the critic forward (22k samples/s vs 35k).

### Reading

1. **The omniscient critic is the one change that helps, and the effect is large for one hour of training**:
   +14 points over the control on the same maps, a training curve that was still rising (62% → 66% in the last
   windows), and the only arm to beat c3 on every map type. It falls just short of the pre-registered 58%-of-games
   bar with the cap games counted as non-wins; counting decisive games it is 65%. The mechanism is the one the
   search study pointed at: fog is where c3's value estimates go wrong, and a critic that sees both views gives the
   policy a far less noisy advantage without changing what the policy sees.
2. **Continuing c3 with its own recipe does nothing** (control 47% of decisive, deterministic). c3's plateau at
   u4854 was real at this compute, not a budget artefact.
3. **Score at the cap** changes nothing in one hour (48% / 47%): the cap games are too few per batch to matter at
   14×14, where only ~8% of training games reach it.
4. **Potential shaping hurt** (43% sampled, 37% deterministic): the policy optimised stars/units/territory at the
   expense of winning while the potential was strong and only partly recovered as it annealed out. Devlin–Kudenko
   preserves equilibria in the limit, not the path; with a strong parent the path matters.
5. **Filter + 1 epoch + EMA** is neutral-to-slightly-positive (+4 over control, inside noise) and makes the learner
   8× cheaper per update. Worth keeping as a cost measure, not as a strength lever on its own.

Recommended next run: the omniscient critic, longer (3 h) and with the filter recipe for learner cost, first on
14×14 from c3 (expect to clear 58% of all games if the curve keeps its slope), then on 18×18 from grown c3 with
the turn clock, where fog on a bigger board should matter even more. Checkpoint archived for that purpose:
`/retained/checkpoints/distributed-final-v1/abl-omni-2-final/` (policy `final.npz`, its critic
`final-critic.npz`).
