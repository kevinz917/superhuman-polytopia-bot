# Stage 0 diagnosis: round-robin of the plateaued 18×18 children (2026-10-20)

**Verdict: flat everywhere.** The five policies form one transitive, nearly level ordering: every pair lands
within 44–56%, a Bradley-Terry fit explains all ten pairs with χ² = 2.1 on 6 degrees of freedom, and there is no
3-cycle. The three later children (run 3 u300, pilot u200, pilot final) sit +24–28 Elo above grown c3 — about
54% pooled over 384 games, a real but tiny edge — and are indistinguishable from one another; run 2 u220 is at
exact parity with its parent. By the Stage 0 rules in `docs/next-run-and-strength-2026-10-20.md`: the children
did not learn counter-strategies that cycle, and the parent-only evaluation was not hiding progress among
siblings. There was no learning signal to find. **Stage 2 (the recipe test on 14×14 from c3) is the answer**;
a PFSP pool is not indicated by these data.

## Setup

Round-robin among five checkpoints, all 10 unordered pairs, on the same fresh 18×18 development maps
(`proposed-v1` mix, seeds 371800 + 64, both seats → 128 games per pair, 1,280 games in all). Both sides sample
from their policy, which is how training measured the children against the frozen parent. Frozen engine
(`cpu_engine fast=False`), turn cap 150. One 32-core Modal container per pair (`arena` entrypoint in
`experiments/modal_distributed_rl.py`), summaries in `docs/ablation-v1/diag-18x18/diag-<a>-vs-<b>.json`.

χ² = 2.06 on 6 degrees of freedom (10 pairs − 4 free ratings): a single ordering explains every pair, with
residuals well inside even the tight paired noise (|z| ≤ 0.84, largest 2.3 points). No directed 3-cycle exists
in the raw scores, strict or otherwise — there is no triple A > B > C > A even before asking whether the edges
clear the noise. **Transitive, and nearly flat.**

The one structure in the data: the three later children are each ~1.3 σ above the parent, and the signal is
consistent. Pooled over their 384 games against grown c3 they score 53.8% ± 1.5% (map-paired se, z = 2.6;
54.9% of decisive games), ≈ +26 Elo. Adding r2u220 gives 52.8% ± 1.2% for all four children. So the children
did move a little past the parent — in the direction training reported — but (a) +25 Elo is a quarter of the
+100 Elo (65%) target and would need ~1,000 games to confirm against any single opponent, and (b) the three
later children, trained with different horizon fixes and stopped at different points, are within ±4 Elo of one
another, i.e. none of the recipe changes between them did anything measurable. Run 3 u300's earlier 60% of
decisive sampled games (seeds 371200 + 64) reads here as 53% of decisive on fresh seeds: that checkpoint was the
best of four candidates on the selection seeds, so the earlier number was inflated by selection.

## 3. Verdict against the Stage 0 rules

- *Children beat each other non-transitively (cycling)?* **No.** Sibling pairs are 44–56%, χ² 2.1/6, no cycles.
  A PFSP-style pool would protect against a failure mode that did not occur here; it is not what these runs lack.
- *Children beat siblings but not the parent (evaluation hiding progress)?* **No.** Children beat neither. The
  parent-relative numbers training reported (49–60% of decisive, sampled) are a fair picture of a plateau at
  parity plus a small drift upward; a pool-based evaluation would have shown the same flat table.
- *Flat everywhere (no learning signal)?* **Yes.** Four runs with three different credit-assignment fixes
  (advantage floor, rescaled γ, turn clock + anchor) all ended within ~25 Elo of their start and of each other.
  The learning signal at 18×18 was too weak for PPO to move the policy anywhere in 40–90 minutes, which is the
  conclusion `docs/long-horizon-credit-2026-10-20.md` reached from the training curves; this is the independent
  confirmation.

**Recommendation:** skip the opponent-pool work, keep the evaluation design (fresh paired maps, both seats),
and go to Stage 2 — the recipe test (zero-sum PBRS, advantage filtering, 1 epoch, EMA, turn clock) on 14×14
from c3, where self-play is known to produce gains, so a null result indicts the recipe rather than the board.
If Stage 2 passes on 14×14 and the same recipe stalls again at 18×18, the remaining lever is capacity, not
opponents.

Side finding worth carrying into every later evaluation: **the first mover wins 69% of decisive games on 18×18**
(664/964, identical for every player and pair: a candidate at seat 1 wins 66% of its decisive games, at seat 2
28%). The both-seats design cancels this exactly; any evaluation or training statistic that does not pair seats
on 18×18 will be dominated by it. It also says the 18×18 opening is lopsided enough that a policy improvement
has to show up mostly as better seat-2 play.
