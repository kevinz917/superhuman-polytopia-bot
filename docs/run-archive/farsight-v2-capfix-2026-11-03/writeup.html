1. farsight v2 (farsight-v2-1v1)

What it is. farsight's recipe: the bighero PPO settings on the real 2.17.3 rules, observation v3 (opponent history planes) and KataGo-style global pooling in the trunk. The original farsight added an unnormalized pooled bias before each block's LayerNorm in all four blocks; one board-wide vector swamped every tile and the policy never learned (0% vs c3 at 1.5 B samples, lr scale stuck at 0.44). Layout 2 normalizes the pooled statistics, adds them after the LayerNorm through a scalar gate initialized at zero, in two of four blocks. Nothing else changed. Trained from random weights for 12 h of learner clock on 4× RTX PRO 6000 plus five GPU-inference actors.

What held up

  • The pooling fix works. The probe that measures the pooled term against the per-tile features climbed from 0.17 at update 200 to about 0.5 by update 1700 and then stayed flat to the end (0.59 / 0.44 at u8860), with the gates parked at 0.25 and 0.20. farsight's final network measured 4.7 to 7.5 on the same probe.
  • Ahead of the control at every equal-update comparison: 72 % vs bighero u5250 at u5140, 69 % vs bighero u10000 at u6860, 80 % vs u10000 at the end. Against the mature Modal bots it reached 21 % vs the champion and 18 % vs hero1 in 12 h.
  • Training was clean throughout: KL 0.005 to 0.007, lr scale 1.0 the whole run, clip fraction 0.05 to 0.08.

Engineering findings from the run

  • The learner's Python intake is the throughput wall, not the actors. With five actors the run ingested ~47k samples/s; adding three actors changed nothing (actor GPUs at 15 %, workers blocked on their send queues, learner inbox full, learner process on one to two cores). The Oct 28 recipe on observation v2 took 120k/s on the same hosts, so v3's larger views roughly halve the GIL-bound intake.
  • A fresh learner is twice as fast. After a spot reclaim at 03:30, the replacement learner plus fresh actors ran ~90k samples/s against the old process's 47k (57 GB RSS, 900 threads by then). A periodic learner restart is a cheap lever for long runs; coded views are the deeper fix.
  • Daytona operations: the org cap is 250 vCPU across all sandboxes, which refused 19 of the final ladder's 36 boxes while round 5's boxes were alive (re-run by hand); the API drops TLS handshakes often enough that box creation now retries; the keeper now defers a round while one is pending and tears the fleet down before the final.

2. Arm B (farsight-v2-capfix) score-at-cap

What it is. farsight v2's config with exactly one change: a capped game pays the score-based terminal reward (cap_score_reward 1.0) instead of a loss for both seats (truncation_reward -1). Same seed, byte-identical random parent. The hypothesis, from the cap-reward design session: loss-at-cap during the first thousand updates taught the from-scratch runs that elimination is the only route to +1, which is why they never learned mid-game city growth; hero-1v1, trained under score-at-cap on the old rules, was two to three times more sample-efficient. Pre-registered gate at about u5140: (a) above 55 % head-to-head against v2's u5140 snapshot, and (b) a growth signature (more builds, city levels still rising at turn 16).

One platform knob differs: stream_views off, after the first two learners died and deadlocked on the documented 4-GPU page-streaming hang. It does not change the learning math. Launched 10:30 local, restarted clean at 11:50, 88 to 93k samples/s throughout.

Verdict on the A/B

  • The pre-registered gate failed at u6200: 22 % against v2's u5140 snapshot and no growth advantage. By the brief's rule, the cap reward is not what separates v2 from bighero, and it is not a growth lever: Arm B builds more but its cities are no higher.
  • The final changes the strength picture. Arm B finished stronger than v2 against every opponent it never trained against (champion 46 % vs 21 %, c3 87 % vs 56 %) and level with v2 head-to-head (61 W / 57 L). Part of that is 33 % more updates; the trajectory against the champion says most of it is the rule. Score-at-cap makes the more general bot; the sibling head-to-head understated it all day.
  • What score-at-cap does buy: decisive games (half the caps), and a bot that generalizes better to opponents it never trained against at this age: 23 % vs champion and hero1 at u6200 where v2 had 8 to 14 %, and parity with v2 against bighero's early snapshots.
  • One caveat on the head-to-head. The ladder scores a capped game as no result, which is the old rule's framing; under Arm B's own objective a capped game it leads on score is a win, and 9 to 24 of 64 games against v2 snapshots capped. The 22 % is a floor, not a fair-rules number, but the deficit in decided games (14 W / 32 L) is large enough that the verdict would not flip.

2b. Arm B segment 2: the equal-sample test against hero-1v1 Nov 3

What it is. Arm B resumed from its segment-1 end for another 11.3 hours of learner clock, to about 7.6 B samples, so it could be compared with hero-1v1's plain trunk at the same sample count under the same score-at-cap reward. The ladder carried hero-1v1's final at 3.9 B, its continuation at update 11500 near 6.9 B and its last checkpoint at 17840, plus the champion, farsight-v2's final, bighero u10000 and Arm B's own segment-1 end.

What happened on the way. The Daytona org ran out of GPU credits in the first hour, which looked like spot churn until the keeper's creates started failing; with two actors left the learner's KL blew up, so the fleet was taken down and the resume point rolled back to update 14,000. Relaunched at 09:00 once the credits were back. The warm restart at the full learning rate then produced 6,000 updates with no net gain (39 % against its own start, self-play pool rate below segment 1's), so at 13:55 the learner was switched to anneal from 1.5e-4 to 4e-5 over the remaining hours; that restart cost 30 more minutes because the actors had to be reconnected. From there the rounds climbed steadily. The hub sandbox expired mid-run and was recreated; the keeper gained retries for refused creates, an iteration-level error guard, and a 3-hour collect timeout after two rounds were lost to slow hosts; the final ladder had to be re-triggered by hand because the resumed segment inherited the previous segment's done marker.

Verdict

  • Equal-sample test: not passed. At 7.6 B samples Arm B is level with hero-1v1's 3.9 B network (48 %) and behind its 6.9 B continuation (39 %). Under the same reward, the pooling-plus-history trunk did not out-learn the plain trunk per sample. The strongest 1v1 network remains hero-1v1's continuation.
  • The segment did improve the bot: 69 % against its own segment-1 end and 56 % against the champion, up from 46 %, almost all of it after the anneal began. A continuation that starts annealed instead of at the full rate would have had about four more productive hours.
  • What the architecture bought is unchanged from segment 1: a large edge over the matched loss-at-cap control (85 % vs bighero u10000) and better generalization than farsight-v2 to opponents it never trained on. It is not a sample-efficiency lever.
  • Caveats: the segment lost roughly 90 minutes and 6,000 updates to infrastructure and the warm-restart mistake; hero-1v1 also had score-at-cap from update zero on observation v2, so the comparison bundles reward timing with architecture.

3. What is known now

  • Strongest from-scratch recipe per update: layout-2 pooling on observation v3 under loss-at-cap (farsight v2). Its final checkpoint is on the volume and resumable.
  • The cap reward is not a growth lever but is a strength lever on this architecture: it did not change city growth, and it produced the bot with the best results against outside opponents of any from-scratch run (champion 46 % at 13k updates). Whether the same holds on bighero's recipe is Arm A's question, running on Curio in another session.
  • Throughput is capped by the learner's Python intake at 47k samples/s on observation v3 (90k with a fresh process). Any run longer than 12 h should either restart the learner periodically or move to coded views, which was a 1.8× lever on Curio.
  • Daytona needs the keeper's new guards (box retries, round deferral, teardown before the final) and a quota budget shared with whatever else the org runs.

4. Options for the next step

A. Continue Arm B from its final checkpoint

Resume u13014 for another 12 h with the same recipe. It was gaining fastest of any run at the end (champion 23 → 46 % over the last 7,000 updates) and is the strongest from-scratch bot against outside opponents. Compare against the champion, hero1 and v2's final each round. A farsight v2 continuation is the alternative if the head-to-head matters more than generality; the two could also run side by side for a clean equal-update answer.

B. Fix the intake first, then run longer

Enable coded views for observation v3 (test view_codec with the history planes on Daytona first), or schedule learner restarts. Doubling samples per dollar matters more than any single 12-h run if the plan is to keep training this line.

A few hours of engineering plus a 1-h smoke (≈ $30); pays back on the first long run.

C. Scale the architecture

A wider or deeper layout-2 trunk from scratch on the fixed intake. The net-size sweep plan from Oct 28 is the template.

≈ $700–800 for a two-size sweep on Daytona.

D. Settle the equal-update question cheaply

Resume farsight v2 from u9779 for the 3,200 updates that bring it to Arm B's 13,014, then ladder both finals against the same references. That separates the rule's effect from the extra updates without a new run.
