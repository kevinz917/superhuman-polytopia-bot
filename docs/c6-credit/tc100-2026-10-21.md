# c6-credit arm C: the undiscounted turn clock (turn_gamma 1.0) on the c5-omni recipe (2026-10-21)

Question: every sparse-win-reward system in the literature (AlphaZero, AlphaStar, DeepNash) trains on the terminal
reward with γ = 1, while `c5-omni-s1` discounts per decision (γ 0.995, λ 0.99 over ~785 decisions per seat per game,
so the opening sees ~2% of the win). Campaign `c6-credit` runs three 1-h continuations of `c5-omni-s1-final`,
identical except the credit clock: A control (per-decision γ 0.995 / λ 0.99), B turn clock γ 0.99 / λ 0.95, **C
(this arm) turn clock γ 1.0 / λ 0.95** — discount only at the seat's own end-turn decisions, none at all here, so
the outcome reaches every decision of a finished game undiminished and λ = 0.95 per turn is the only horizon. Ledger
`runs/c6-credit/tc100/budget.json` (cap $35, not committed); spec `runs/c6-credit/tc100/spec.json` (copied to
`docs/c6-credit/tc100/`). Branch `claude/c6-credit-tc100-2754d3`.

## Is γ = 1 supported end to end?

Checked before launch, no fix needed:

- `LearnerConfig.validate` (`src/polytopia_bot/rl/distributed.py`) only requires `turn_gamma` and `turn_lambda`
  together; nothing bounds γ below 1. The actor builds `SeatTrajectories(games, segment, turn_gamma, turn_lambda,
  turn_clock=True)` whenever `turn_gamma` is set, so 1.0 (a float, not None) takes the turn-clock path.
- `gae` / `_sequence_terms`: the per-decision discount is `g = where(end_turn, γ, 1)` and the trace
  `gl = where(end_turn, γλ, 1)`. With γ = 1 that is `g ≡ 1` and `gl = 0.95` at end turns: the backward recursion
  `A_t = δ_t + gl_t A_{t+1}` is a convex mixture of the outcome and the critic's values, so returns stay in
  `[−max(1, |V|), +max(1, |V|)]`; nothing divides by `1 − γ`.
- Truncated games (round cap 100, 10,000-action limit): `episode_outcome` returns the engine's zero rewards with
  `terminal = False` (`truncation_reward` is None), and the actor bootstraps the omniscient critic's value of the
  final state for both seats (`boot` from `critic_value(critic, omniscient_features(...))`). That path does not
  read γ at all, so a capped game bootstraps exactly as it does under γ 0.99 (the last turn's returns are the
  bootstrap value; under 0.99 they are 0.99× it).
- Learner side: advantages are normalised per batch by `max(std, advantage_floor = 0.05)`; the omniscient critic
  regresses the (bounded) returns with a plain squared loss and no value normalisation; UPGO is off (no second
  return estimator). Nothing assumes γ < 1.

New unit test `tests/test_distributed_rl.py::test_turn_clock_undiscounted_gamma_one`: config validation, λ = 1 ⇒
every return equals the outcome, λ = 0.95 ⇒ finite and bounded with the last turn's returns equal to the outcome and
`G_7 = 0.95·1 + 0.05·V_8` across an end turn, truncation bootstrap `0.3` vs `0.99·0.3` under γ 0.99, and a

Reading of the training-time record:

- **Sampled play never beat the parent by all games.** Decisive share drifted from 50% to 55–57% in the middle
  of the hour and back to 52% / 45% in the last two windows, while the share of capped games against the parent
  jumped from 1–7% in the warm-up to 20–26% after it. For scale, `c5-omni-s1` itself in its first hour from c3
  (updates 251–300 of its log) was 51.8% of all games vs frozen c3 with 22% capped and 67% of decisive, so the cap
  share is ordinary for a fresh critic, but the decisive share here (52–57%) never left the 50s, and by all games
  the policy sat at 41–46% vs its parent for the whole second half of the hour. The control arm's and arm B's
  tables are the comparison; a 1-h continuation of a 6-h checkpoint at the same lr is expected to drift at
  sampled temperature, so this is not by itself a loss.
- **The fresh critic under γ = 1 reached a value std of 0.62**, twice c5's 0.28–0.30 and still rising slowly at
  the end (0.36 after the warm-up, 0.50 at 100, 0.60 at 200, 0.62 at 300). That is the expected scale change: the
  undiscounted return of a decided game is ±1 at every decision of every turn, not ±γ^k, so the critic's
  regression target has roughly the full outcome spread from the first turn on. Value loss 0.003–0.006 (c5: similar),
  value mean +0.09 to +0.15 (the learner seat's self-play returns are positive because the sampled policy beats the
  frozen parent's and the snapshots' games are 15%; `return_mean` tracks `value_mean` within 0.01).
- **Advantages never hit the learner's floor:** the raw per-batch advantage std was 0.065–0.115 (window minima
  0.065 in the warm-up, ≥ 0.085 after), against `advantage_floor` 0.05. `c5-omni-s1`'s own log
  (`/retained/…/c5-omni-s1-final/training.json`) shows 0.059 in its warm-up and **0.039–0.044 for the rest of its
  six hours, i.e. below the floor**: under the per-decision clock the normaliser was dividing by the floor, so its
  normalised advantages had std ≈ 0.8, whereas this arm's had std 1 in every batch. The undiscounted clock thus
  raised the raw advantage scale 2.5× and, as a side effect, the effective policy step by ~25% relative to the
  recipe it was compared against (the control arm inherits c5's floor-limited scale).
- Entropy 1.99–2.3 after the warm-up (c5 end: 2.32), approx KL per update 0.0077–0.0080 against the 0.03 target
  (never an early epoch stop, clip fraction 0.09–0.10), Warrior share of recruits 20–27% (c5 final 25%).
- Self-play cap rate 16–21% after the warm-up (c5 last 2 h: 19%), i.e. the undiscounted clock did not make
  self-play games longer; only the games vs the frozen parent capped more often.

### Pre-registered reading: **loses**

- Holds (≥ 50% of all games vs the parent): **no**, 41.8%.
- Wins (CI lower bound > 50%): no.
- Loses (CI upper bound < 50%): **yes**, upper bound 46.5%.

By decisive games alone the final is at parity with its parent (53.8%, CI including 50%), and it wins from the
first seat (59.6% of decisive) while losing from the second (47.4%); what costs it the all-games score is the 22%
of games that reach the round cap (57 of 256; `c5-omni-s1-final` vs c3 capped 8–10%, and vs the 1-h OMNI checkpoint
15%). Every map type is ≤ 50% of all games; waterworld (73% of decisive) and drylands (33%, 10 games) are the
extremes. The comparison that answers the campaign's question is against arm A (same hour, per-decision γ) and
arm B (turn clock γ 0.99), in the parent session's readout: a 1-h continuation at lr 2e-4 with a fresh critic can
fall below the parent on all three clocks (c5's own first hour from c3 was only 54% of all games vs c3 after
starting from c3's strength), so the control's number is the baseline for this one.

- Critic value std under γ = 1: 0.62 at the end of the hour vs c5's 0.28–0.30 (and arm B's expected ~0.3): the
  critic reached a steady scale (0.60 → 0.62 over the last 100 updates), but twice the discounted one, as the
  returns themselves are twice as spread.
- Advantages stayed above the normalisation floor in every batch (raw std 0.085–0.115 after the warm-up vs floor

  the control's at the same lr — a confound to keep in mind when reading the three arms, and an argument for
  normalising by the real std (floor 0.01, as the 18×18 recipe does) if the clock is tried again.

## What went wrong

Nothing on this arm: no preemptions, no orchestrator replay (14 containers on the app at every check, 13 workers +
orchestrator), all 12 actors at 16 processes × a full core, the learner on its 3,600-s clock, the ladder in one
pass. The in-container CPU check needs `modal container exec <id> -- python -c …` (the `--` is required, and the
image has no `ps`/`top`; `/proc/stat` deltas work: `runs/c6-credit/tc100/cpucheck.py`). Modal's billing report lagged
the 17:00 UTC row by over an hour, so the "first billing row" rule could only be applied after the run (≈ $21/h).
`scripts/ledger_spend.py --write` was not used; the rows were recorded with `budget.record` by hand. At 11:21 the
control arm's app showed 27 containers (13 extra), which looked like the c5-l18-omni replayed-orchestrator pattern;
that session was told and its app was back to 13 by 11:39.
