# c6-credit arm B: the turn clock at γ 0.99 / λ 0.95 for 1 hour from c5-omni-s1-final (2026-10-21)

Campaign `c6-credit` asks whether the credit clock of [the c5 recipe](../c5-omni-long-2026-10-21.md) (per-decision
γ 0.995 / λ 0.99 over ~785 decisions per seat per game, so the opening sees ~2% of the win) is holding the
strongest 14×14 bot back. Three arms continue `c5-omni-s1-final` for one hour, identical except for the credit
settings: A control (per-decision 0.995 / 0.99), **B this arm** (turn clock γ 0.99 / λ 0.95: discount only at
the seat's own end-turn decisions, 1 within a turn, as in [the 18×18 run](../c5-18x18-omni-2026-10-21.md)), C turn
clock γ 1.0 / λ 0.95. The parent session compares the three reports. Ledger `runs/c6-credit/tc99/budget.json`
(cap $35, shared `runs/` symlink, not committed); result files in `docs/c6-credit/tc99/`.

## What went wrong

- Nothing broke. No preemption, no orchestrator replay, no duplicate learner (14 containers all hour); the ladder
  ran detached and its file was on the volume before the client printed it.
- The actor fleet took 8 min to be fully placed (6 of 12 containers at +6 min), so the learner's first ~8 min of
  clock ran on half a fleet: ~35 updates fewer than a fleet that is up at once (c5's 1-h ablation arm reached
  307 updates with the same shape, this run 332 with a faster learner, so the loss is small).
- The billing-rate check could not be made at 20 min as pre-registered (hourly rows lag the hour); for 1-h runs
  the check has to rely on the previous arms' measured rate ($22/h) or on `modal app` task counts.
- `final.json`'s metadata block still prints the per-decision `gamma` / `gae_lambda` defaults (the checkpoint
  writer copies the structured-ppo schema); `protocol.json` is the record of what the learner actually ran
  (`turn_gamma` 0.99, `turn_lambda` 0.95).

## Reading

1. **The turn clock at γ 0.99 did not improve on the parent in one hour.** Deterministic on fresh maps the
   continuation is 101–94 in decided games (51.8%, CI 44.8–58.7) and 39.5% of all games, i.e. a tie in decided
   games and a loss under the pre-registered all-games rule. The sampled training-time record vs the frozen
   parent told the same story all hour (51.3% of decisive, 53.4% over the last half hour, no upward trend).
2. **The policy did move.** Approx KL 0.008 per update for 272 policy updates, entropy 2.1–2.2, and a visible
   change of plan (Cloak share 21% → 35%, Defender 21% → 14%): the hour was not wasted on a frozen policy, it
   spent its learning on a strategy shift that is not stronger than the parent's against the parent.
3. **The fresh omniscient critic fitted the turn-clock targets quickly.** Value std 0.46 by update 100 and 0.50–0.52
   from update 200 on, tracking the return std within 0.01, value loss 0.003. The turn-clock returns are ~1.7×
   wider than per-decision c5's (0.28–0.30) because γ 0.99 per turn delivers 60–70% of the terminal reward to the
   opening instead of ~2%; the critic is not the bottleneck of this arm.
4. **Cap games are the cost of the mirror.** 24% of ladder games ran to the 100-turn cap (mean 100 turns) against
   8–10% when c5 played c3, and the candidate was capped more often as second player (34 vs 27). Any arm that
   is only at parity with its parent will show the same shape, so the parent session should compare the three
   arms on decided games and on the cap rate, not only on the all-games rule.
5. **What it says for the campaign.** Compared with the control arm A (same hour, per-decision clock) and arm C
   (γ 1.0), this arm is the middle point of the credit sweep. If A also sits at parity in decided games after an
   hour, one hour is simply too short to separate credit schemes on a 6-h-trained parent and the comparison needs
   either a longer continuation or a weaker common opponent (raw c3) where the three finals can be ranked with
   less cap noise; if A pulls ahead and B/C do not, the per-decision clock is the better fit for 14×14 and the
   turn clock's benefit on 18×18 came from the longer games there. Seat split, map table and unit mix above are
   there for that comparison; the ladder seeds 389200–389327 are arm B's own, so a cross-arm paired comparison
   needs a rerun on shared seeds (≈ $1.5 per pair).
