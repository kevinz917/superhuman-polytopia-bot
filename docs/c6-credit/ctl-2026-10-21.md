# c6-credit arm A (control): the c5-omni-s1 recipe for one more hour, per-decision credit (2026-10-21)

Question of campaign c6-credit: does credit assignment on the sparse win reward matter for the 14×14 omniscient-critic
recipe? Three 1-h arms from the same parent, identical except the credit settings: **A control** (this report; the
recipe's per-decision γ 0.995 / λ 0.99, which gives the opening ≈ 2% of the win over ~785 decisions per seat), **B**
turn clock γ 0.99 / λ 0.95 and **C** turn clock γ 1.0 / λ 0.95 (the literature's choice for terminal-reward games;
`docs/credit-assignment-review-2026-10-21.md`). Arm A is the baseline the other two are read against. Ledger
`runs/c6-credit/ctl/budget.json` (cap $35 incl. the ladder; copied to `docs/c6-credit/ctl/budget.json`).

- Updates 1–60 are the critic warm-up: every non-critic gradient is zeroed, so the 52% of the first window is the
  parent playing itself at temperature 1 (the sampling-noise baseline) while the fresh omniscient critic fits (value
  std 0.25 → 0.29 by update 100; the entropy figure of the first window is low because the first games are short
  openings). The low cap share of the first window is a windowing effect too: capped games are long and end later.
- From update 61 the policy moved (approx KL 0.005–0.008, clip fraction ≈ 0.08) and the sampled record vs the parent
  never rose above parity: 48% → 47% → 42% → 46% of decisive games, 45.2% over the last half hour, with the cap
  share of vs-parent games climbing from 6% (51–100) to 22–28% (101–308). Entropy drifted up from 2.3 to 2.5
  (c5 held 2.1–2.5), the critic's value std settled at 0.27–0.30 (c5: 0.29–0.31), the Warrior share stayed at
  20–22% (c5 final: 20%). Nothing diverged; the policy just did not improve on its parent in the hour.
- Raw (pre-normalisation) advantage std after the warm-up: 0.037–0.048, mean 0.043, last 50 updates 0.039, i.e.
  **below the `advantage_floor` of 0.05 in every batch**, so this arm's normalised advantages had std ≈ 0.8–0.9 and
  its effective policy step was ≈ 15–20% smaller than an arm whose raw std clears the floor (the arm-C note reports
  0.085–0.115 under γ = 1, std 1 after the floor). c5-omni-s1 itself trained at 0.039–0.044, so the control is faithful
  to the parent's regime; the comparison across arms carries this confound.
- Contamination from the duplicate learner (11:06–11:27): the duplicate resumed from the original's `latest.npz` at
  ≈ update 70 and, for 21 min, wrote `latest.npz`, `training.json` and the `update-100`/`update-150` snapshots
  into the same run folder as the original. Both learners keep their snapshot pool and history in memory, so the
  original's training data (its own actors, its own snapshots served from memory) and its `training.json` (rewritten
  in full at every checkpoint; the archived file is monotone 1–308 with a single clock) are the original's. The
  files the duplicate wrote were overwritten by the original's later checkpoints; only the duplicate's sampled games
  during those 21 min (vs `parent` and its own pool) went into the shared status rows, not into the original's learner.
  One original actor container (16 processes) re-resolved to the duplicate after a process restart and fed it
  instead of the original; after the cancel its processes did not all re-join (187 of 192 connections for the last
  25 min, ≈ 3% less throughput). The run otherwise matches the c5 recipe.

### Pre-registered reading

- **Holds (≥ 50% of all games vs the parent): no.** 31.6% of all games, 43.1% of decisive games.
- **Wins (CI lower bound > 50%): no. Loses (CI upper bound < 50%): yes**, 35.9% < 50% on all games; on decisive
  games alone 43.1% [36.1, 50.3] (Wilson) just touches parity.
- Expectation was "slightly above parity, since the c5 curve was still rising at 6 h". The control is **below parity
  by ≈ 36 Elo (score 0.449, caps as draws) or ≈ 135 Elo if caps count as losses**, and the sampled training record
  (47% of decisive over the hour, falling) agreed with the ladder in direction. So one more hour of the unchanged
  recipe with a fresh critic did not continue c5's curve; it set the policy back. The two candidate causes are both
  shared by all three arms, which is what makes arm A the right baseline: (i) the critic reset — the omniscient
  critic is re-initialised and fitted for 60 updates (13 min), then the policy trains against a critic that is
  13 min old rather than 6 h old (c5-omni-s1 trained its critic for 2,212 updates); (ii) the matched-opponent
  regime — 5% of games against a frozen copy of itself plus 15% against snapshots that are within an hour of it, so
  the sampled vs-parent signal is at parity by construction and the self-play cap share rose to 20–26%.
- The deterministic ladder's cap rate vs the parent (27%, mean 54 turns) is far above c5's 8–10% vs c3 (40 turns):
  two near-equal 6-h bots stall each other, and the arm loses the decisive games it does get (43%), on both seats
  (seat-2 is the weak seat, 33% of decisive).

## What went wrong

1. **Orchestrator replay (11:06–11:27).** The known Modal failure from c5-l18-omni recurred: the preempted
   `orchestrate` container was replayed and spawned a duplicate learner and fleet on the same run id; the duplicate
   learner's `finally` marked the shared registry key stopped when cancelled. Handled by restoring the registry entry
   first, cancelling with `terminate_containers=True`, and a 50 ms polling guard that re-wrote the entry the instant
   the stop mark appeared (one restoration; no original actor saw it). Cost ≈ $8 and 5 actor processes. The arm-C
   session spotted it from the container count (27 vs 14) 14 min after it began; my own 5-min and 10-min checks were
   before the replay and the next scheduled look was the 11:25 billing check. **Fixes still pending in the launcher**
   (listed in the c5-18x18 report): make `orchestrate` refuse to spawn when `registry[run_id]` is live, make the
   learner's `finally` mark `stopped` only when the entry is its own, and check `modal container list` counts per app
   at every status check (the only visible tell).
2. The first cancel attempt lost 2.5 min to an SDK signature (`FunctionCall.from_id` takes no `environment_name`;
   set `MODAL_ENVIRONMENT=staging`), during which the duplicate kept training.
3. `scripts/analyze_c5_training.py` hard-coded the frozen opponent's name `c3`; it now takes `--opponent NAME` (the
   sibling arms made the same change on their branches, under `--frozen` on arm B).
