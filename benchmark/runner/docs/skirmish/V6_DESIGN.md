# Local v6: evaluation breadth and fairness

Version: `polytopia-local-v6`. Historical v5.1 results are retained. This revision adds opt-in exact-round overrides and updates shared runtime/authoring adapters; it does not rescore historical cohorts. The execution backend is the existing Colima VM with offline Docker coder/referee containers. Trusted Mini-SWE-agent controllers and subscription CLI-proxy requests run on the host. There are no Modal execution calls and no paid API fallback.

The benchmark measures a model/provider/harness combination under an active wall clock. API latency and retries count; explicitly logged CPU scheduler queue waits do not. Equal `high` settings and response ceilings do not establish equal inference compute. Token receipts, output-cap hits, API latency, tool time and excluded scheduler waits are reported. Missing subscription dollar receipts mean unavailable cost data, not a zero-cost invoice.

Three equal-time tracks distinguish a continuous independent attempt from four-stage authoring without feedback and four-stage authoring with feedback. Feedback uses a frozen, seat-balanced, mixed-reference rotation. Comparing feedback with the segmented control removes the stage-boundary confound, while independent vs segmented separately measures it. Multiple-comparison and small-pilot uncertainty remain.

The full default has five independent builds, ten shared hidden layouts per family, and at least five peer layouts per family. The intended final comparison can raise layouts to 20–30 per family and builds to 10. Pilot manifests explicitly permit fewer. All attempts count; failed compilations score scheduled zeroes. Bootstrap intervals use 4,000 crossed independent-build/shared-layout resamples with fixed family weights. Layout draws are shared across models/tracks, but independent build indices are not falsely paired. Reports include direct model/track differences and the descriptive matchup matrix, cycles and secondary Elo. Intervals condition on the fixed reference panel and are not multiplicity-adjusted.

The six-reference candidate panel retains the historical weak/intermediate Wasm bots and hero, and adds two explicit hero/style blends (25% hero turns with cavalry, 50% hero turns with naval) and a deterministic defensive siege script. Blends and scripts consume only the public observation. They are newly constructed calibration candidates: names do not establish intermediate strength, distinct strategic behavior, or absence of panel saturation. Their code/config/weights are digest-pinned before authoring. Calibration reports all results; future panel changes require a new protocol, never reweight this cohort after inspecting hidden scores.

Core games use exactly 200 complete rounds, ending before round index 200 starts, and 40,000 shared actions with 4 trillion fuel per guest. The exact-round override is applied only by the v6 environment constructor; legacy engine defaults and archived results retain their behavior. Both feedback and final guards are 1,800 seconds. Engine caps are declared benchmark draws; guest faults forfeit, and infrastructure timeouts remain unresolved and block a complete score. Separate reasons distinguish round/action caps, fuel exhaustion, decision timeouts, guest exits and infrastructure failures. Per-seat action attribution exposes shared-cap pressure.

The cap calibration uses disjoint reserved maps at 100/150/200/300 rounds, fixed reference matchups, both seats and equal action/fuel settings. Generated final bots receive a separate horizon-sensitivity test after freezing. Neither diagnostic changes primary scores. 200 is a preregistered candidate, not a claim of empirical optimality. Calibration checks completion, outcome/rank changes, family effects, action counts, runtime and safety-limit failures. Small maps/weak scripts can still stalemate; there is no score/critic adjudication or decisive-only filtering. The real-engine preflight exercises exact round boundaries and an intentionally small shared action budget, recording attribution rather than claiming exploit resistance.

Budget curves use frozen current-source samples at 25/50/75/100% of the independent attempt and stage endpoints for the staged tracks. No best-of selection or extra feedback is used. Snapshot capture time is inside the independent coding clock; actual sample time is recorded. Compilation happens outside it. Early submission ends the attempt; missing later fractions are missing, not invented results.

Separate transfer diagnostics cover pangea, archipelago and highlands on 11/14/18 boards. Authors are told to read `visible_state.board_size`. Hero is not silently adapted to another board size; transfer uses the size-independent scripted reference. Targeted engine-valid snapshots cover last-city capture, a one-hit kill and the start of a knight chain. These single-decision tests are narrow diagnostics, not proof of complete tactical competence. All games retain deterministic action-chain replay audits. Replay validates this implementation, not equivalence to the commercial game; commercial-rule parity remains a distinct gate.

## Deadline recovery validation

The local controller now writes atomic conversation receipts before the first request and after every message. Cutoff cancels the Mini worker and its HTTP subprocess group; deadline exceptions are not API-retry candidates. A blocked first request was cut off at two seconds, its conversation resumed in a second stage, and the second stage submitted successfully in the actual VM sandbox. HTTP requests use the remaining active stage budget instead of an independent 60-second timeout. The interrupted pre-fix pilots are retained with explicit infrastructure invalidations and no hidden scores.

## Corrected evaluation panel v6.1

Before hidden scores existed, a static review and trusted trace count found that the cavalry blend voluntarily disbanded units (266 disbands in 13 original-feedback games). The calibration candidate now excludes routine disband. A separate, predeclared 90-game round robin on three reserved calibration layouts (both seats) produced 89 native conclusions and one cap draw. The corrected naval-50 reference won 6–0 against the old intermediate and lost 0–6 against hero, an initial middle-strength screen rather than a high-confidence strength estimate. The cavalry blend remains a weaker style reference; the names do not imply equal difficulty.

All twelve authored bots are retained from `v6-proxy-20261031c`, including its original uniform feedback, with exact source/Wasm/protocol hashes. `scripts/regrade_skirmish_v6.py` performs zero authoring calls, copies every frozen artifact by model/track/replicate, independently rebuilds them, and allocates a new hidden plan for the corrected evaluation-only panel. It never mixes old outcomes or returns corrected-panel feedback to an author. The resulting evaluation revision is `reference-panel-v6.1`; the original authoring protocol remains linked rather than retrospectively rewritten. Future fresh v6 cohorts use the corrected panel for their declared feedback too.

Pilot intervals are explicitly omitted when fewer than two independent builds or two layouts per family are available; a zero-width bootstrap from one observation is not presented as a credible confidence interval. The full five-build/ten-layout configuration retains crossed bootstrap intervals and paired-map contrasts. Generated-bot horizon sensitivity spans the scripted siege reference, naval-50 middle reference, and full hero at 100/150/200/300 rounds; each diagnostic declares its actual coverage.

## Final pilot and authoring-tool follow-up

The completed corrected-panel pilot contains 2,880 full replay-audited games, 36 single-decision tactical checks and 36 declared compilation-loss outcomes from one intermediate snapshot. All 44 captured source snapshots independently rebuilt. There were no unresolved outcomes. The separate generated-bot horizon sweep changed 1/216 matched outcomes between 100 and 300 rounds, and 0/216 between 150 or 200 and 300 rounds. No per-track model ordering changed across the four tested horizons. This supports retaining the preregistered 200-round cap for this pilot, while leaving broader horizon independence unproven.

After scoring finished, two future-authoring fixes were applied without regenerating any submission or outcome: public feedback now whitelists visible fields and names the actual `/feedback/round-N/00.jsonl` sandbox paths, and the v6 coder image explicitly gives its offline protocol checker the same four-trillion fuel allowance as evaluation. The legacy checker default remains one trillion. The original pilot used that older helper and host-relative report paths; those limitations remain disclosed in its summary. The original feedback, protocol hashes, evaluation allocation and frozen evaluator archive are preserved.
