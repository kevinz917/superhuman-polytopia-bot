# Replicate the Critic Win Before Scaling

Two days and roughly **$700 of Modal spend** across the 2026-10-19/20 experiments produced exactly two levers that moved strength with any confidence: one-turn lookahead search at inference (**+56 to +117 Elo leak-free**, CIs clear of zero, reproduced on two bots and two map sets) and an omniscient critic in training (**54% [50.0, 58.2] vs c3 against the control's 40%**, one seed, one hour, CI touching 50%). Everything else tested on those days was null or negative: critic-only search, Gumbel root search over sub-actions, one-round search distillation, three 18×18 self-play runs plus a turn-clock pilot, continuing c3 unchanged, cap-score reward, potential shaping, and advantage filtering ([internal notes](docs/ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md), [TTS-u3142](docs/test-time-search-2026-10-19.md)). The through-line is value estimation under fog: both wins bypass the critic's blindness to the opponent (search looks ahead; the omniscient critic sees both views at training time), while every failed lever operated through the same noisy critic. The literature the notes assembled says the two biggest failures were failures of form, not idea: the distillation used hard labels from a teacher that overrides the policy 87% of the time by margins below its own score noise, which is exactly the ExIt/Gumbel "chosen-action target" failure mode, and the critic-side win matches AlphaStar's single largest RL ablation (22% to 82% best-response win rate from opponent observations in the value function). The recommendation is a **$325 first tranche** whose centrepiece is a two-seed, three-hour replication of the omniscient critic against a matched control, flanked by three cheap, high-information checks (a $10 frozen-critic re-fit of the existing distillation data, a $45 exploiter run that doubles as an AlphaStar-style best-response replication of the critic effect, and a $20 belief-prior upgrade to the leak-free searcher), and a **$265 conditional second tranche** (soft-target distillation, a global-pooling capacity arm, an 18×18 omni-plus-turn-clock run, and the winner-speed bonus) gated on the first tranche's results. An evaluation of the omniscient-critic bot with and without leak-free search is already running; its outcome decides whether the serving path is "omni policy plus parallel searcher" or whether the value head the search relies on must be refreshed first.

Internal citations are repo-relative paths; several live only in the worktree `.claude/worktrees/sharp-goldstine-e442a1` or on the branch named in the notes' source key ([internal notes](docs/test-time-search-2026-10-19.md)). Elo CIs are paired-map bootstrap 95% intervals; 256-game ladders resolve about ±4.5 points, 200-game search arms about ±35–40 Elo, 1,000-game student evaluations about ±13 Elo.

**Experiment 1 is the only proposal that can change the project's direction, so it gets the largest share.** The omniscient critic arm was pre-registered to pass at 58% of games vs c3 with a CI above 50% and five points over control; it landed at **138–75–43, 54% [50.0, 58.2], 65% of decisive, winning on all ten map types**, against a control at 102–116–38, 40% [36, 44] ([READOUT](docs/ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md)). The JSON lower bound is 49.99%, so the CI technically includes parity. It did this on **80M samples versus the control's 113M**, because actors ship the opponent's view and run the critic forward (22k vs 35k samples/s), which argues the effect is understated at equal samples; and it climbed monotonically per 50-update window after warm-up, 52% → 55% → 58% → 63% → 66% of decisive sampled games, which is the signature of learning rather than noise ([READOUT](docs/ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md)). The design keeps the arm's recipe exactly (separate critic on both seats' views, 60 critic-only warm-up updates, c3's PPO settings, 1×H100 + 12 × 16-vCPU actors at TF32) and changes only duration and seed: two omni seeds at 3 h, one control at 3 h so that "longer" is tested on both sides, and one omni-plus-filter+EMA arm because the readout recommended that combination and the filter is 8× cheaper per learner update while being neutral on strength ([READOUT](docs/ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md)). Report equal-sample checkpoints alongside the equal-wall-clock ladder, because the arm will finish with roughly 240M samples against the control's 340M. The pass rule is deliberately stricter than the original: both seeds must clear 58% independently, since the Generals.io authors saw two seeds of their final recipe sit on an early plateau for 16–20 h before taking off, which means a single seed at 3 h can mislead in either direction ([Straka et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23348v2)). At ~$20 per arm-hour, four arms for three hours is $240, plus about $10 of ladders and smokes; Modal preempted two of five learners in the last program, so hold a 10% contingency ([ARMS](docs/ablation-v1/arms-2026-10-20.md)).

**Experiment 2a costs $10 and settles the distillation post-mortem's main hypothesis.** The distillation report's stated "likely cause" for losing the search gain (students +40–45 under leak-free search versus c3's +104 [+69, +141] on the same maps) is that the refit critic was trained toward a max-over-16-noisy-lanes value and became optimistic; the authors wrote that a distillation meant to keep search working "should leave the critic alone (value_coef = 0, or a value anchor to c3); this was not tested" ([DISTILL](docs/search-distillation-2026-10-19.md)). The existing dataset (23.37M labelled decisions, all 16 lane scores recorded) supports this re-fit with zero new generation; training one variant cost under $1 last time, a 1,000-game raw evaluation about $2, and a 200-game search-on-top arm about $4 ([DISTILL](docs/search-distillation-2026-10-19.md)). The result is informative either way: if search-on-top recovers to ≥ +80, the policy labels were harmless and only the critic target was wrong, which licenses Experiment 2b; if it stays near +40, the policy itself drifted to states the critic ranks badly, and distillation must track candidate-ranking quality rather than policy loss. The literature backs the hypothesis: MuZero Reanalyse keeps value targets on n-step returns, lowers the value loss weight to 0.25, and regressing the value head to the search's root value measured worse than TD-5 (median 115.3% vs 126.6% at 20M frames); KataGo keeps game outcome as the main value target and uses search values only as low-weight auxiliary heads ([MuZero Unplugged](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294), [KataGo methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)).

**Experiment 7 answers two open questions for $45.** No human, independent bot, or exploiter has ever played c3; the strength chain (league96 → u3142 +410 → c3 +150–290) "has no external meaning" and nothing supports a "superhuman" claim ([NEXT](docs/next-run-and-strength-2026-10-20.md)). Training a fresh learner from c3 against 100% frozen deterministic c3 for one hour measures exploitability directly, with AlphaStar's 70% exploiter reset threshold as the reference for "clearly exploitable" ([TStarBot-X restating AlphaStar](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13729)). Running it twice, once with the symmetric critic and once with the omniscient critic, converts it into the cleanest possible replication of the Stage-2 win: AlphaStar measured its value-function ablation exactly this way, as best-response win rate against fixed opponents "to avoid multi-agent dynamics", and got 22% without opponent information versus 82% with it ([Vinyals et al. 2019](https://gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/model-free/alphastar/2019-vinyals.pdf)). Self-play confounds critic quality with opponent drift; a fixed opponent does not. Pre-register the comparison as omni-exploiter minus symmetric-exploiter on the same 256-game ladder, with +5 points as the noise floor the ablation program already uses.

**Experiment 4 is engineering-bound and cheap to evaluate.** Removing the fog-of-war leak cost −58 [−114, −6] and −69 [−122, −19] on u3142, −81 and −148 on c3 classic, and −58 on the map mix, and the authors state that the gap is "leak + belief error" because the determinizer's prior is crude: "No enemy units are imagined in the fog", and on variant maps the fog is filled from the classic generator, so "mix-map leak-free numbers are, if anything, low" ([TTS-u3142 addendum](docs/test-time-search-2026-10-19.md), [TTS-c3](docs/test-time-search-c3-2026-10-19.md)). Three changes address the named defects: fill fog with the correct map generator, place enemy units in fog by a simple prior (the searcher's own unit count by turn, near known enemy territory), and score each candidate under two or three sampled worlds and average rather than one. The third is the cheap version of MAPLE, which aggregates several sampled world states in one tree and gained **+291 Elo on Phantom Go and +136 on Dark Hex** over a single-determinization PIMC baseline, and it attacks the strategy-fusion defect that Long et al. identified as PIMC's structural weakness ([MAPLE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24139), [Long et al. 2010](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/7562/7562-13-11092-1-2-20201228.pdf)). Evaluation is cheap: the c3-v2 sweep ran ten 200-game arms for $29, so four arms with smokes is about $20 ([TTS-c3](docs/test-time-search-c3-2026-10-19.md)). Compute per turn will roughly scale with determinizations, which the 16-core parallel searcher (0.95–1.2 s/turn today for the robust leak-free setting) can absorb ([SPEED](docs/search-speed-2026-10-19.md)). The pass criterion is a paired delta, because the absolute +104 [+69, +141] on the mix is too wide to tell from +130 unpaired.

**Experiment 2b is the literature's prescribed fix for the +0 Elo distillation, but it is only worth running if 2a shows the critic was the problem.** Seven students trained on hard "search-chosen action" labels all landed within ±3 Elo of c3 on 1,000 games each; search overrode greedy in 87% of plans with a median gain of 0.023, about the median spread of the 16 lane scores (0.018), and no student predicted override labels better than c3 itself (0.444–0.447 vs 0.445) ([DISTILL](docs/search-distillation-2026-10-19.md)). Every reference lineage that measured it found hard labels inferior: ExIt's tree-policy target beat the chosen-action target by **+50 ± 13 Elo at identical top-1 accuracy** (47.0% vs 47.7%), because the information lives in the margins over non-chosen moves; Gumbel MuZero's "simple" argmax loss is strictly worse than its completed-Q loss at every simulation budget, and plain MuZero fails to learn 9×9 Go at all with 16 or fewer simulations while Gumbel learns with two ([ExIt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439), [Danihelka thesis](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)). The 16 sampled whole turns are, up to with/without replacement, the Gumbel-top-k candidate set with m = 16, so the target is a sequence-level softmax over candidates of logits + (c_visit + max N)·c_scale·q_normalised, with unsearched turns at zero advantage, and Sampled MuZero supplies the proposal correction when candidates are drawn at a temperature other than the policy's ([Sampled MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06303)). The existing generator does not record non-chosen lanes' action sequences, so new data is needed; the earlier run produced 16.6k games for $81, so ~12k games is ~$60, with training, raw evaluation and search-on-top adding ~$20 ([DISTILL](docs/search-distillation-2026-10-19.md)). Two controls are mandatory: a hard-label arm with the critic frozen (isolating target form) and a c_scale → 0 arm, because self-distillation of a near-identical teacher can produce apparent gains that are pure regularisation ([Das & Sanghavi 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13304)). Weight samples by KL(prior ‖ target) as KataGo does, so that the 87% of low-margin states contribute little ([KataGo methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)). Expectation should be modest: the per-decision signal is mostly noise, and the honest answer of a noise-aware target to a noisy teacher is a small update.

**Experiment 5 tests whether 0.5M parameters is the ceiling, with the one architectural change the literature ranks highest.** The control arm's result (continuing c3 with its own recipe for an hour gives 40% against c3, 47% of decisive) says the recipe has zero or negative marginal value at this capacity, and four 18×18 configurations all ended +3 to +28 ± 19 Elo over their parent, transitive and flat ([READOUT](docs/ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md), [DIAG](docs/ablation-v1/diagnosis-18x18-2026-10-20.md)). The network is 4 residual blocks × 64 channels, "about the budget Jones (2021) found necessary for 9×9 Hex", and Neumann & Gros find compute-optimal size scales as N_opt ∝ C^0.62, so at ~35–50k samples/s on 4×H100 it is almost certainly undersized ([NEXT](docs/next-run-and-strength-2026-10-20.md), [Neumann & Gros 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.00849)). KataGo's ablation puts global pooling at 1.60× training-time equivalent (1,329 → 1,153 Elo when removed), second only to auxiliary ownership/score heads, and Ben-Assayag found that a small local-receptive-field CNN "declined most as boards grew" while global-context architectures transferred to boards 2–3× larger ([KataGo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.10565), [Ben-Assayag & El-Yaniv 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.08387)). A zero-initialised pooling branch preserves c3 at step 0 exactly as `network.grow` did for the 18×18 blocks ("identical choices, log-probs and values to c3 on 640 states") ([L18](docs/rl-18x18-2026-10-19.md)). This must sit on top of the omni recipe, since it is the only recipe known to learn from c3; three hours is the minimum for new capacity to be used.

**Experiment 6 is the 18×18 run the readout recommended, gated on the critic replicating.** The 18×18 diagnosis is now clean: the per-decision discount collapsed credit (0.995^2400 ≈ 6e-6 versus 0.995^890 ≈ 0.01 on 14×14), the turn-clock pilot fixed the critic (value std 0.25–0.28, matching c3's 0.28) but left the policy at parity (deterministic 37–34–25), so credit assignment was "a real defect, not the only one" ([HORIZON](docs/long-horizon-credit-2026-10-20.md)). The missing ingredient on the readout's account is the critic's fog-blindness, which the omni arm addressed on 14×14 ([READOUT](docs/ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md)). Costs have changed: TF32 convolutions pass the parity gate (max |log r| 6.5e-3, no row outside the PPO clip) and make the 18×18 learner epoch 3.28× faster, so the learner stops being the bottleneck; 8-vCPU actor containers deliver 1.66× the samples per actor dollar at 18×18, though that bench showed 1.6× run-to-run host variance ([GATE](docs/ablation-v1/precision-gate-2026-10-20.md), [BOTTLE](docs/rl-18x18-bottlenecks-2026-10-20.md)). The pre-TF32 fleet cost $51.5/h; with the learner faster and smaller actor containers, ~$35/h is a reasonable estimate, so 2 h ≈ $70 plus a Stage-0-style ladder. Two cautions belong in the pre-registration: omni actors run the critic forward and ship the opponent's view, which will cut 18×18 actor throughput below the 2.0–3.4k samples/s per container measured for the plain recipe, and the first mover wins **69% of decisive 18×18 games** (664/964), so ladders must be seat-balanced and reported by seat ([L18](docs/rl-18x18-2026-10-19.md), [DIAG](docs/ablation-v1/diagnosis-18x18-2026-10-20.md)). Starting from Experiment 1's 14×14 child rather than grown c3 is the mixed-size transfer that KataGo and Ben-Assayag rely on, and the net already runs on any board size.

**Experiment 3 is the cheapest proposal and the one with the worst prior.** Three reward interventions aimed at stalled games have now failed: cap-as-draw trailed control (71% vs 85% decisive) and was stopped; cap-score ±0.5 changed nothing in an hour because only ~8% of training games reach the cap at 14×14; potential shaping over stars/units/territory/cities hurt (37% vs control's 40%, 43% sampled) despite Devlin–Kudenko's equilibrium guarantee, which "preserves equilibria in the limit, not the path" ([MAPS](docs/map-variants-2026-10-19.md), [READOUT](docs/ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md)). The winner-speed bonus in the uncommitted handoff differs in form: only the winner's reward rises with speed, the loss stays −1, so the stall-when-losing incentive the Generals.io author blames on symmetric discounting ("a discounted agent stalls when losing and rushes to bank wins when ahead") does not arise, and the spread between fastest and slowest win (0.3) is under a sixth of the win-loss gap ([handoff](docs/winner-speed-bonus-handoff-2026-10-20.md), [Straka blog](https://kam.mff.cuni.cz/~straka/blog/generals.html)). Its closest published analogue is KataGo's small score utility (c_score 0.4–0.5) acting as a tie-break toward finishing ([KataGo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.10565)). The strongest 4X-genre evidence, however, is that a sparse terminal reward with γ = 1.0 and a hard cap scored 0/0 reached superhuman Generals.io play, and that shaping's early lead (~+150 Elo at 24 h) shrank to ~+90 at 48 h ([Straka et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23348v2)). The honest expectation is a shorter-game effect without a strength effect, so the pass criterion is "not weaker", and the run is a rider on whichever launcher goes out next, not a standalone priority. Note the handoff's own caveat that values stop being win probabilities, which touches the search scorer.

Deliberately not proposed: search inside training at KataGo's 25% playout-cap fraction. Searched decisions run at 4.6 per core-second against ~156 raw decisions per core-second in actors, 34–40× fewer samples per dollar, so campaign 3's 1.3B samples would become ~30M searched samples for the same money ([HORIZON](docs/long-horizon-credit-2026-10-20.md)). Reanalyse-style offline relabelling of stored decisions (Experiment 2b's data path) is the cost-aware substitute the literature points to, with 80% of MuZero's policy-target updates coming from re-searched stored states ([MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265)).

## Two levers survived; eleven premises did not

The confidence hierarchy of the two days is worth stating plainly, because it determines what can be built on. **High confidence** attaches to the search results (200 games per arm, paired CIs clear of zero, reproduced across u3142 and c3 and across sessions: c3's leak-free d=1 gain of +104 [+69, +141] on the mix was re-measured on a fresh seed block in the distillation study), to the parallel searcher's bitwise equivalence over 84 whole turns, to the TF32 gate on 20,480 real actor rows per board, to the distillation null (seven students, 1,000 games each), and to the 18×18 plateau (a 1,280-game round-robin in which a Bradley–Terry fit explains all ten pairs with χ² = 2.1 on 6 degrees of freedom, no 3-cycle, and all four children within +3 to +28 ± 19 Elo of their parent) ([TTS-c3](docs/test-time-search-c3-2026-10-19.md), [DISTILL](docs/search-distillation-2026-10-19.md), [SPEED](docs/search-speed-2026-10-19.md), [GATE](docs/ablation-v1/precision-gate-2026-10-20.md), [DIAG](docs/ablation-v1/diagnosis-18x18-2026-10-20.md)). **Medium confidence** attaches to the omniscient critic (+14 points over control, one seed, lower CI bound 49.99%), to the hidden-information share of the search gain (one paired comparison per setting, with a crude belief prior that cannot separate leak from belief error), and to the PBRS harm (one coefficient, one schedule, one seed). **Low confidence** attaches to per-map-type orderings (14–30 games each, ±0.2), to the 8-vCPU cost advantage (single benches with 1.6× host variance), and to any absolute strength claim ([READOUT](docs/ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md), [TTS-u3142 addendum](docs/test-time-search-2026-10-19.md), [GATE](docs/ablation-v1/precision-gate-2026-10-20.md)).

The search picture is sharper than the headline numbers suggest. Leaky d=1 adds **+125 [+87, +168] on the u3142 mirror and +162 [+127, +200] on the c3 mix**; leak-free keeps roughly half on u3142 and classic maps (+56 [+21, +92], +82 [+30, +139]) but most of it on the mix c3 was trained on (+104 of +162) ([TTS-u3142](docs/test-time-search-2026-10-19.md), [TTS-c3](docs/test-time-search-c3-2026-10-19.md)). Robust search (64 averaged replies, sequential halving) was "the first setting where more compute keeps paying off" when leaky (+172 [+118, +233] vs E3; +200 [+162, +244] on c3 classic) but adds only +13 [−26, +54] over plain leak-free d=1 on the mix at 2.5–2.9× the compute: "averaging replies mostly helps when the replies are real" ([TTS-u3142](docs/test-time-search-2026-10-19.md), [TTS-c3](docs/test-time-search-c3-2026-10-19.md)). That asymmetry is the reason Experiment 4 targets the belief prior rather than more replies. Critic-only scoring at T=1 failed by the optimizer's curse (predicted gain per turn grew +0.013 → +0.032 from K=4 to K=64 while realised strength stayed +14 to +28, overrides on 65–95% of turns) and the Gumbel-style root search over distinct first sub-actions lost 179 of 200 games (−413 Elo) with turns collapsing to 9 sub-actions, which the authors attribute, plausibly but untested, to a critic "confidently wrong on states the policy never reaches" ([TTS-u3142](docs/test-time-search-2026-10-19.md)). The value-head probes on u3142 explain the mechanism: the critic is a good ranker from turn 10 (AUC 0.92–0.995) but a linear fit on true-state material explains R² 0.79–0.86 of it, it does not infer the enemy star bank (R² ≤ 0.08), it is near-blind to which opponent it faces (Brier 0.22 vs a constant's 0.025 in turns 0–9 against league96), and in capped games "the value stays near 0 from start to end", so v ≈ 0 means "even or headed for the cap" ([PROBES](docs/policy-probes-2026-10-19.md)).

Two engineering results change the economics of everything above without claiming strength. The parallel searcher plays bit-identical turns to the lockstep leak-free robust searcher in 0.95–1.2 s on a 16-core container versus 9.4–10.7 s on one core, at about $0.00025 per searched turn, and more than 16 cores cannot help because the critical path is ~280 lane-steps per turn ([SPEED](docs/search-speed-2026-10-19.md)). TF32 convolutions with fp32 heads give 1.92× learner throughput at 14×14 and 3.28× at 18×18 ([GATE](docs/ablation-v1/precision-gate-2026-10-20.md)). The first makes search serveable; the second makes the 18×18 learner no longer the bound and is why Experiment 6 is priced at ~$35/h rather than $51.5/h.

## The literature says the forms were wrong, not the ideas

**The omniscient critic is the best-supported idea in the whole program, and its form matters.** AlphaStar fed the opponent's observations to its value functions "to decrease the variance of the value estimates", training-only, and the Fig. 3k ablation shows 22% best-response win rate without opponent information versus 82% with it, the largest single RL effect in the paper ([Vinyals et al. 2019](https://gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/model-free/alphastar/2019-vinyals.pdf)). Honor of Kings put a 560-dimensional block of invisible opponent information into its value net and reports an Elo gain; PerfectDou's perfect-information critic beat its imperfect-critic ablation and reached DouZero's strength with ~10× fewer samples ([Ye et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12692), [Guan et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16406)). The theory that governs the form is Baisero & Amato's: a state-only critic V(s) is biased for a history-conditioned policy and can actively punish information gathering (their Dec-Tiger example gives history value ≈ 13.9 against state values ≈ −16.2), while the history-state critic V(h, s) is unbiased; MAPPO's SMAC ablation found the same thing empirically, with the agent-specific global state (global state plus the agent's own observation) beating both the global state alone and concatenated local observations ([Baisero & Amato 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.11674), [Lyu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01221), [Yu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01955)). The Stage-2 arm fed the critic both seats' views, which keeps the acting seat's own observation and is therefore the endorsed form for a feed-forward policy; Experiment 1 should not change it. One refinement is worth a note rather than an arm: Informed Asymmetric Actor-Critic shows any state-dependent privileged signal is admissible as long as the history is kept, and in its synthetic study a reward-relevant subset beat the full state (AUC ≈ 1.23e5 vs 1.19e5), with the warning that "adding irrelevant or rapidly varying features can hurt value estimation" ([Ebi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26000)). The 60-update critic warm-up the arm used is three times the 20-update figure that recent systems report when a critic's inputs change, which is on the safe side ([Le Critique](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16739), [ADEPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19182)). OpenAI Five is the counter-example by omission: a shared-trunk observation-only critic sufficed for Dota given ten months of compute, which makes the privileged critic an efficiency lever rather than a necessity, exactly the regime a few-hundred-dollar budget lives in ([Berner et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680)).

**The distillation failure is a textbook case, and the textbook has a fix.** The hard label on a search-chosen turn is ExIt's chosen-action target and Gumbel MuZero's "simple policy loss"; both lineages measured it as strictly worse than a soft target over all searched actions, and Grill et al. explain why few-simulation visit counts are poor targets (a ratio of small integers that cannot express fine-grained preferences, and newly found good actions that take many simulations to show up) ([ExIt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439), [Grill et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12509)). Gumbel's guarantee holds only "if the visited actions are correctly evaluated", so critic noise is the direct threat; the design's answers are a bounded advantage scale (c_visit = 50, c_scale tuned so the target spreads logits by roughly the critic's true signal, with the beam-rider ablation showing that over-trusting q hurts in a partially observable game) and Sequential Halving to concentrate evaluations on survivors ([Danihelka thesis](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10167022/2/ivo_danihelka_thesis.pdf)). CRR and AWR give the same prescription from the offline-RL side: weight labels by exponentiated or thresholded advantage, which, applied to a teacher whose advantages are mostly noise, correctly produces a small update; MuZero Unplugged's offline Atari ordering (BC 53% → CRR 156% → search targets 265% median) is the quantitative case that soft, advantage-aware targets are where the gain lives ([CRR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15134), [MuZero Unplugged](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294)). On the imperfect-information side, no reference system distils a determinized one-ply argmax: ReBeL and Student of Games distil equilibrium-solved subgame outputs, DeepNash distils nothing and still reached 84% against Stratego experts, and the determinized argmax is the configuration most exposed to strategy fusion ([ReBeL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13544), [DeepNash](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15378)).

**The long-horizon levers mostly confirm what the 18×18 campaign found the hard way.** The Generals.io 2026 agent, the closest published analogue in genre and budget (fog-of-war grid 1v1, ~730-turn episodes, 4 GPUs × 7 days), used γ = 1.0 with GAE λ = 0.9, sparse terminal reward, truncation at a hard cap with reward 0, an EMA policy that led the last iterate by 65 Elo on average, and bootstrapped the win signal with a spawn-distance curriculum rather than shaping ([Straka et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23348v2)). Its advantage filtering (top 25% by |advantage|) doubled iterations per wall-clock and ended 150–250 Elo higher at equal time but level at equal frames, which is the same "cost measure, not a strength lever" reading the ablation arm earned here ([Straka et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23348v2), [READOUT](docs/ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md)). The 18×18 diagnosis maps onto the plateau theory cleanly: Balduzzi's Hodge decomposition says a transitive plateau with near-zero curl means no learning signal rather than cycling, and the round-robin's χ² = 2.1 with no 3-cycle is that case, so league machinery (PFSP, exploiters) is the wrong remedy and Czarnecki's advice to "start agents higher on the top" through stronger priors (search, architecture) is the right one ([Balduzzi et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.02643), [Czarnecki et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.09468), [DIAG](docs/ablation-v1/diagnosis-18x18-2026-10-20.md)). Jones's Hex scaling gives the budget rule for board growth: minimum compute for a fixed strength rises 4–7× per board-size increment and the random-to-perfect gap grows by 500 Elo per increment, so 18×18 at the 14×14 budget was never going to be cheap, and a flat ladder at 2× compute is a pipeline plateau rather than insufficient compute ([Jones 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.03113)). The 14→18 transfer in Experiment 6 is the mixed-size regime KataGo trains in (37.5–50% of games on 19×19, the rest on 9×9–18×18) and the "train on small, play the large" regime where a size-agnostic architecture beat an AlphaZero trained ~10× longer on the large board ([KataGo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.10565), [Ben-Assayag & El-Yaniv 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.08387)). One sobering comparison: Generals.io's decisive advantage was a GPU-resident JAX simulator at ~42.8M frames/s on one H200, three orders of magnitude above this project's 35–50k samples/s CPU-actor pipeline; the L4 benchmark (vmap runs every `lax.cond` branch, ~90 ms fixed latency per step) says that path is closed for this engine shape, which is why the proposals above buy signal quality rather than throughput ([Straka et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23348v2), [SPEED](docs/search-speed-2026-10-19.md)).

## The pending omni-plus-search eval sets the serving path

The evaluation already running, the omniscient-critic bot with and without leak-free search, is not re-proposed here, but its result should be read against three branches before tranche 2 is launched. The c3 reference points are **+104 [+69, +141] for leak-free d=1 on the mix** and **+40–45 for the distilled students**, whose critic had been refit toward an optimistic target ([TTS-c3](docs/test-time-search-c3-2026-10-19.md), [DISTILL](docs/search-distillation-2026-10-19.md)). If the omni bot gains roughly +100 or more, the training-side and inference-side levers stack: the policy improved on its own and whichever value head the searcher used still ranks candidates well, so the serving path is the omni policy plus the 16-core parallel searcher, Experiment 4 rises to priority 2 because the belief prior then limits the strongest deployable bot, and Experiment 6 inherits a parent worth scaling. If the gain falls to the +40–50 range, the policy has moved to states the search's value head ranks badly, or has already absorbed part of the one-turn lookahead; either way the critic used by search must be refreshed before search is served, Experiment 2a's lesson on critic handling becomes the first thing to apply, and the candidate-ranking AUC of the critic on held-out searched states should be added as a first-class metric for every subsequent run, as the search-improvement literature recommends when a search gain is critic-driven ([MuZero Unplugged](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06294)). The report of that eval must state which value head the searcher scored with: the archived checkpoint ships `final.npz` and `final-critic.npz` separately, and the omniscient critic itself cannot run leak-free at test time because it needs the opponent's view, so only a leaky run with it would be an upper bound rather than a deployable number ([READOUT](docs/ablation-v1/readout-2026-10-20.md)). A third outcome, a search gain larger than c3's, would indicate the omni training also sharpened the policy-side value head and would justify co-training the policy's own value head against the omniscient critic's targets as a cheap auxiliary in Experiment 1's follow-up; that is an inference, not something the notes measured.

## Conclusion

The two days changed the problem statement. The project no longer lacks throughput, credit assignment, or map coverage; it lacks a value signal that survives fog, and both levers that worked are ways of routing around that lack. That reframing is what makes the $325 first tranche coherent: Experiment 1 asks whether the training-side route is real, Experiment 7 asks the same question in the cleanest setting the literature offers while measuring exploitability for the first time, Experiment 2a asks whether the inference-side route can be transferred into weights without wrecking the critic that makes it work, and Experiment 4 asks how much of the inference-side route was ever real skill. Each can fail cheaply, and each failure closes a branch rather than a budget.

The tranche-2 gates are the discipline the earlier program lacked. The distillation pilot's +40 on 100 maps and run 3's 60% of decisive were both selection artefacts that cost real money to unwind, and the ablation's pre-registered 58% bar is the only reason the omni result is called borderline rather than a win. Holding every second-tranche run to a stated gate, a stated seed count, and a paired comparison on fixed map blocks costs nothing and is the difference between a $500 spend that produces a stronger bot and one that produces another plateau diagnosis.
